## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

The cooperation premium compensates a private cost. The cost can differ depending on B’s action, so a premium between the two costs does not identify A’s action without its beliefs. Delta is in money-equivalent utility units and pi is a probability in [0,1]; B remains self-regarding. Warm glow describes the benefit from A’s own cooperation, rather than a direct weight on B’s money.

## Explanation steps

1. Start from CC(a,a), CD(d,c), DC(c,d), DD(b,b), with c>a>b>d. Add delta only when A chooses C: CC utility (a+delta,a), CD(d+delta,c); the other row remains (c,d),(b,b). Money itself has not increased by delta.
2. Against B=C, compare a+delta with c: A strictly cooperates iff delta>c−a. Against B=D compare d+delta with b: cooperation iff delta>b−d. Equality is indifference in the corresponding comparison.
3. Below both thresholds, D is strictly dominant (the lecture’s “egoist” behavior label, even if a small positive premium exists). Above both, C is strictly dominant (“dominant-strategy altruist”). Intermediate premiums are called “best-response altruists”, with two possible orderings: c−a<delta<b−d yields C against C and D against D; b−d<delta<c−a yields D against C and C against D. c>a>b>d alone cannot select an ordering.
4. At a simultaneous choice A does not observe B’s current action. With pi=Pr(B=C), expected C utility is pi(a+delta)+(1−pi)(d+delta), expected D utility is pi c+(1−pi)b. Subtract: DeltaU=delta−[pi(c−a)+(1−pi)(b−d)]. Choose C if positive, D if negative, tie if zero. This is the finished notes’ algebraic derivation, not an estimated belief model.
5. B’s own monetary payoffs are unchanged; c>a and b>d keep D strictly dominant for B. Correctly anticipating a selfish B means pi=0, so compare delta to b−d. In the first intermediate ordering A then defects; in the second it cooperates. Therefore the lecture’s broad claim that any intermediate type guarantees observed cooperation requires qualification.
6. In the original a=16,c=20,b=12,d=8 matrix both costs are 4: delta<4 gives D, delta>4 gives C, delta=4 ties against either B action. No nonempty intermediate interval exists. A’s C at delta>4 is paired with B’s D, not mutual cooperation.
7. The separate reciprocity explanation requires a belief about kindness in a simultaneous game; a trustee instead observes the prior transfer in sequential trust. The notes supply no quantitative reciprocity threshold, full study methods or numerical cooperation rate. Social preferences can increase welfare, conditional on incentives, preferences and beliefs, rather than always doing so.
