## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

Own and other payoffs use identical monetary units; rho and sigma are dimensionless. The active weight is selected by monetary inequality. A preference can value another person’s income positively when ahead and negatively when behind, so one unqualified “unselfish” label loses predictive detail.

## Explanation steps

1. Select $w=\rho$ for $x_1\ge x_2$, otherwise $w=\sigma$. Then $U_1=wx_2+(1-w)x_1=x_1+w(x_2-x_1)$. Equality yields $U_1(x,x)=x$ from either expression, so the pieces meet.
2. With $0<\rho<1$ and $\sigma<-\rho<0$, ahead utility is $x_1-\rho(x_1-x_2)$ and behind utility $x_1-(-\sigma)(x_2-x_1)$. The disparity amounts are nonnegative in their own regimes; both are penalties. Since $-\sigma>\rho$, the behindness penalty per unit is larger. This does not remove the value of own income.
3. Fix utility $k$: $wx_2+(1-w)x_1=k$. For $w\ne1$, $x_1=(k-wx_2)/(1-w)$, so with vertical $x_1$ and horizontal $x_2$ the slope is $-w/(1-w)$. At zero weight it is horizontal; at $0<w<1$ downward; at negative weight upward. At $w=1$ only $x_2$ matters and the curve is vertical. At $w>1$ the own coefficient is negative and the usual positive-own-weight interpretation fails; algebra gives an upward slope. A positive weight alone does not imply the plotted downward shape.
4. Selfishness: both weights 0. Altruism: both positive, sometimes $0<\sigma<\rho$, with no universal upper bound imposed by the lecture’s general definition. Spite: both negative. Inequality aversion: ahead positive/behind negative with the taught stronger-penalty condition. Source figures are qualitative, not estimates.
5. To decide between allocations calculate utilities; an unequal high-payoff pair may outrank a low equal pair. Costly UG rejection can be consistent with a sufficiently strong behindness penalty, but aggregate rejection does not identify each individual’s parameters.

## Misconceptions and correction

Signs: applying rho when behind, reversing axes, saying all positive weights slope down or all equal allocations win. Correction: select monetary regime before substituting; retain the (1-w) own coefficient; compare utilities of both pairs. Hint1: which monetary payoff is larger? Hint2: solve fixed-U equation for vertical x1. C1 is fresh repair only.

A sign is a tentative hypothesis about the error until the learner’s reasoning supports it. Score the initial response before giving the corrective explanation or either hint.
