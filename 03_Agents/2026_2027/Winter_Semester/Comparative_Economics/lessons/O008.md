## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

Economic institutions are rules/enforcement governing property, contracts, entry, skills, land, credit and innovation incentives. Political institutions distribute power to choose/change those rules and constrain officials; state capacity and effective power matter beyond constitutional text. A patent can protect an invention yet commercialization also needs entry, workforce and finance. The chapter compares opportunities offered to skilled ambitious people, not national innate motives or a complete accounting of every fortune. Privately owned firms can still enjoy protected monopoly; privatization alone is not competitive entry.

## Explanation steps

1. Trace post-independence power: US constitutional constraints developed from assemblies but accepted slavery and excluded many voters. Mexican elites opposed threatening Cádiz reforms and supported independence retaining privileges; Iturbide concentrated power. Instability can weaken rights/services, but Díaz shows stability/export growth can coexist with favoritism/expropriation. Ask who benefits, not merely whether rulers are stable.
2. Link power to financial rules: the authors say electoral constraints could frustrate US politicians’ bank-monopoly attempts, while favored Mexican insiders sustained concentration. Same profit motive, different rule constraints. Banking access changes who can finance/commercialize ideas; patents alone are insufficient. US/mexican bank counts/assets use different dates and contexts, so not a matched causal test.
3. Interpret Gates: skilled workforce, finance, markets/rights helped Microsoft, and public antitrust action constrained a powerful firm. This is not absence of US monopoly attempts.
4. Interpret Slim: prior investment/turnaround success plus Telmex privatization supplied protected market access; consortium control of51% voting stock equaled20.4% total stock, not51% of all shares. Political access, entry/finance barriers, payment terms and regulatory resistance shaped monopoly profit. Amparo’s rights-protecting origin differs from its strategic use. A different US litigation context constrained his side in the Dallas dispute; do not infer a universal national legal ranking.
5. Connect feedback: incumbents receiving rents can defend rules, while market opportunity/power also changes over time. US slavery, restricted voting and Indigenous dispossession; Mexican export growth/reforms/conflict qualify simple good/bad stories. Historical repression counts from distinct reports/estimates cannot be merged as one precise count. Narratives illustrate a theory, not identify institutions as the exclusive cause of prosperity.
