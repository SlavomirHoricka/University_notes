## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

A best response is the action with the preferred personal payoff given the other action. A dominant action is best regardless of the other’s action. Nash equilibrium means no player gains by switching alone. Smaller prison terms are better; larger output/utility numbers are better, and cells are A,B. In cooperation/PD, each can free ride even when mutual cooperation benefits both. Coordination means the best action depends on expectations about others; multiple consistent patterns can be equilibria and welfare can differ. Driving-side conventions exemplify coordination, not a PD. Pure-action comparisons suffice; mixed equilibria, probabilities and risk-dominance calculations are not taught in the notes and are excluded.

## Explanation steps

1. Hold B’s choice fixed, compare A’s payoffs in that column, then hold A fixed and compare B’s row payoffs. Never compare sums to infer an individual best response.
2. In the note PD: if B silent A prefers0 years to.5; if B confesses A prefers2 to10. Symmetry gives both confession dominant and (confess,confess) Nash, although both prefer.5 years each. Mutual silence is not stable against a lone defection.
3. In the note stag hunt, against stag A prefers4 to3; against hare A prefers3 to0, similarly B. Both stag and both hare are equilibria; stag4 each exceeds hare3, but a lone stag hunter gets0. There is no dominant stag or hare action; uncertainty can favor safe hare without making it uniquely inevitable.
4. Map only supported assumptions to applications: public goods/commons can create contribution/use externalities, but a large country can benefit enough from its own emission cuts that inaction is not always dominant. A threshold protest can make joining worthwhile when an individual is pivotal and benefit exceeds participation cost. Free riding is a difficulty, not a proof of zero turnout.
5. Organizations, participation/elections, local user rules/monitoring/conflict resolution/sanctions can sustain cooperation. Enclosure can protect resources while harming previous users. Local Ostrom-style governance needs trust/participation and is not universally successful. Standards, conventions/focal points or joint infrastructure/private-investment efforts can coordinate expectations, but switching costs/cultural variation/credibility remain. Do not infer welfare from equilibrium alone.
