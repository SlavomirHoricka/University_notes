## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

Hold-up requires a prior sunk relationship-specific investment that weakens the investor’s outside option; the other side can later demand worse terms. Sunk means the cost cannot be recovered by walking away. Asset specificity means substantially less value outside this relationship. Commitment is more general: a promise wanted now may become privately unattractive to keep later, without any specialized investment. Time inconsistency means the preferred action changes between promise and performance. Distinguish ex ante investment choice, ex post production choice and whole-project accounting. Let I be investment dollars, q units, c variable dollars/unit, p dollars/unit; project profit is (p−c)q−I and break-even promised price c+I/q. Ex post compare future payoffs; subtracting sunk I from both choices does not change their ranking.

## Explanation steps

1. Before investment, require expected revenues to cover both variable and initial costs. At the note’s I=10,000, q=1,000, c=20, p=30, project result=(30−20)1,000−10,000=0; p_BE=30.
2. After investment, with no other buyer/use and no further costs, an offer p'=25 produces operating surplus(25−20)1,000=5,000. Rejecting yields zero future surplus, so producing is better.
3. Overall loss after accepting is5,000−10,000=−5,000, versus−10,000 if rejected. The source’s “operating loss” expression is a project loss; it cannot justify rejecting a positive future surplus. At exactly variable cost the two choices tie; above it producing wins under the specified assumptions.
4. Anticipating p'=25 before investment yields−5,000, so the supplier declines the investment in this simple setting. An outside buyer/use could weaken this hold-up threat; no hidden information is necessary.
5. An enforceable price contract supplies a fallback but incompleteness/litigation can allow pressure. Alternative buyers/uses improve outside options. Integration puts both stages inside one firm but may reduce production efficiency or require unavailable capital. Credible norms can restrain opportunism only with sanctions.
6. In sequential bicycle delivery/payment, either first mover fears the other will renege. This is commitment without a special investment. Legal enforcement, witnesses or credible self-binding change later incentives; mutually preferred renegotiation remains possible. A mechanism’s enforcement function does not make violence or extortion desirable.
