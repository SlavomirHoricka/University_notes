## Motivation and explanation

A short-financed delivery resembles a loan: buy the full cash bond PP at time0 and receive full invoice IP at delivery. With simple annual r and n days, the initial cash grows to PP(1+rn/365). If coupon C is received m days before delivery and reinvested at this same r, receipts are IP+C(1+rm/365). Equating sides, collect r terms: r(PPn−Cm)/365=IP+C−PP. A nonzero denominator therefore gives r=(IP+C−PP)365/(PPn−Cm). No coupon sets C=0, yielding (IP−PP)365/(PPn). These are implied annual repo returns under the stated model, not a compounded project IRR. The finished-note source's printed3.57% does not exactly match its given inputs: using43/180 accrued coupon yields about3.61742%; the note exposes the mismatch. Prepared examples specify consistent full inputs, coupon dates and365 convention. CTD maximises comparable eligible IRR; actual borrowing costs, fees and cash-flow timing decide realised profit. No actual basket in notes means no actual CTD can be named.

## Misconceptions and correction

A positive yield is a return before its funding leg. Write the repayment identity first and keep the actual repo rate separate; a purchase price comparison alone loses coupons and the delivery factor. Observe the learner's reasoning before diagnosing: a sign or omitted word alone is tentative. Score before help.
