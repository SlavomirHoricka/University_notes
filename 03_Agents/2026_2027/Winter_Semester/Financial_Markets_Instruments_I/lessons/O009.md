## Motivation and explanation

An index quote has no money scale until multiplied by the contract multiplier. A minimum tick is a permitted quote increment; tick value is multiplier times that increment. For fixed long N, G=N×multiplier×(F_end−F_start); reverse the sign for a short. Closing ends the position; holding instead substitutes EDSP as the final endpoint. Do not add the whole held payoff to a closed payoff. Margin return divides trading G by the stated initial collateral, whereas contract exposure follows the multiplier and index level. Simple annualisation multiplies that fraction by 365/elapsed days; it magnifies short-horizon leverage and is not a prediction. EDSP is fixed by the contract's observation/trim rule: in the finished note's hypothetical example a sample of observations is taken and extremes removed. The specified benchmark is distinct from an arbitrary quote. Real contract terms require verification outside this prepared illustration.

## Worked example

O009-W1 / item_version 1, worked_example. **Prompt:** Hypothetical SIFT-style index contract: multiplier €10 per index point, minimum tick 0.5 point, initial margin €1000 per contract. A long 20-contract position opens at 5825; it could close at 5812.5 after 5 days, or instead remain open to cash EDSP 5810. Compute tick value, closing signed P/L and percentage return on initial margin, simple annualised closing return using 365 days, and the separate held-to-settlement total. Explain why these are alternative paths, what EDSP does, and whether the return is an index investment yield or repeatable annual forecast. Interpret the multiplier/margin and explain the meaning of a trimmed-sample EDSP, without asserting current exchange terms.

**Steps and interpretation:** One tick = €10/point ×0.5 point = €5 per contract. Closing G=20×10×(5812.5−5825)=€-2500; initial deposited margin €20000, so return -12.5%, annualised -912.5% by simple scaling. Held G=20×10×(5810−5825)=€-3000; this alternative includes the whole opening-to-EDSP move, not the already-closed trade plus settlement. A cash-settled contract pays a difference using EDSP, not delivery of index stocks. EDSP is a rule-defined sample benchmark (the finished-note example trims extreme samples), not any chosen last quote. Multiplier scales exposure; margin is collateral, not index notional. Margin leverage/simple annualisation does not establish an underlying yield or a repeatable forecast. These given specification values are pedagogical, not current market terms.

## Misconceptions and correction

An answer adding two terminal payoffs suggests endpoint confusion. Draw a fork at opening: one branch closes, the other holds. A very large annualised figure can be arithmetically correct but its base is collateral and its repeated-return assumption unjustified.
