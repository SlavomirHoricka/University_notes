## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

Derive the slope disturbance decomposition and conditional/unconditional mean, identify the role of each first four assumption, and explain the limits of repeated-sample unbiasedness. The question is what the available information lets us calculate or conclude, and which extra conditions the conclusion needs.

Unbiasedness means $E[\hat\beta_j]=\beta_j$ across repeated samples. Conditioning on X fixes weights/denominator. It does not fix disturbances or set each realized disturbance to zero. The first four named assumptions suffice; homoskedasticity and normality are not required for this mean result.

## Explanation steps

1. From the linear population equation subtract sample means: $y_i-\bar y=\beta_1(x_i-\bar x)+(u_i-\bar u)$.
2. Insert into Sxy/Sxx; cancel beta 1*Sxx/Sxx. Since centered x sums0, the term involving ubar vanishes: $\hat\beta_1=\beta_1+\sum(x_i-\bar x)u_i/S_{xx}$.
3. Conditional on the whole sample X, Sxx and centered weights are fixed. Thus $E[\hat\beta_1\mid X]=\beta_1+S_{xx}^{-1}\sum(x_i-\bar x)E[u_i\mid X]$. Random sampling plus observation-level zero conditional mean supply $E[u_i\mid X]=0$.
4. The mean error term is 0. Iterated expectation gives $E[\hat\beta_1]=E[E[\hat\beta_1\mid X]]=\beta_1$. For all coefficients, the note's matrix decomposition gives the same conditional argument $E[\hat\beta\mid X]=\beta+(X'X)^{-1}X'E[u\mid X]=\beta$.
5. Linear parameters allow decomposition; random sampling supports conditioning on the full sample; no perfect collinearity allows finite inverse/positive denominator; zero conditional mean eliminates mean errors. Homoskedasticity controls variance, not this mean; normality supports exact inference later.
6. Realized estimation error generally remains nonzero. An unbiased estimator can have large dispersion; one coefficient, residual orthogonality or a high fit cannot certify its repeated-sample property.

## Worked example

O003-W v1, hypothetical fixed X=(1,2,3), beta 1=2, Sxx 2. A realized u=(1,0,3) gives centered weighted sum(-1)(1)+0+(1)(3)=2; slope 3. This single error of +1 does not refute a model whose repeated-sample conditional u means are0. Under those mean assumptions E[slope|X]=2+(-1*0+0*0+1*0)/2=2, then E[slope]=2. Compare realized u with its conditional expectation: no step requires all realized u to vanish.

## Misconceptions and correction

Sign: expectation of ratio treated as ratio of expectations without conditioning, or claims unbiasedness makes each error0. Correction: fix X to make the denominator and weights constants; exogeneity sets conditional disturbance means, not realizations, to0. Hint1: what is fixed conditional on X? Hint2: rewrite the expectation as a weighted sum of E[ui|X].

Score the original response before help. One ambiguous error only suggests a tentative misconception; establish it only from explicit reasoning or repeated diagnostic signs. Explain the relevant prepared step and its consequence; never label the learner from confidence or a single slip.
