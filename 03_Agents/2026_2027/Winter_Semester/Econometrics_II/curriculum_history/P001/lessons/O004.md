## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

Derive the probability limit from centered OLS, state sampling/moment/nondegeneracy and orthogonality conditions, and discriminate finite-sample unbiasedness from concentration in probability. The question is what the available information lets us calculate or conclude, and which extra conditions the conclusion needs.

Consistency is $\hat\theta_n\to_p\theta$: for every fixed epsilon>0, P(|estimate-target|>epsilon)→0. It concerns a sequence indexed by n, not monotonic improvement of every realized sample. Probability limit names the value around which the sequence concentrates; it may differ from the intended parameter.

## Explanation steps

1. Use $\hat\beta_1=\beta_1+\frac{n^{-1}\sum(x_i-\bar x)(u_i-\bar u)}{n^{-1}\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2}$, obtained by centered substitution and common division by n.
2. Under independent random sampling and finite moments sufficient for the law of large numbers, sample covariance tends to Cov(x, u), sample variance to Var(x). Require positive finite population Var(x) so the ratio is valid. Then plim slope = beta 1+Cov(x, u)/Var(x).
3. If covariance0 the slope limit equals beta 1; otherwise larger samples concentrate at the wrong target. Zero conditional mean is sufficient, stronger than needed for these moments. The weaker condition is E[u]=0 for the intercept and Cov(xj, u)=0 for every nonconstant regressor, alongside other model/sampling/moment/rank conditions. Cov(1, u)=0 says nothing about E[u].
4. Finite-sample unbiasedness concerns an expectation at each n. Consistency concerns concentration. Neither follows from one estimate or an unqualified large n.
5. For an unbiased sequence, Chebyshev bounds P(|estimate-target|>epsilon) ≤Var(estimate)/epsilon²; if variance→0, the probability bound→0. Unbiasedness alone does not require variance shrinkage. A finite-sample biased sequence can be consistent if error/bias disappears with n and its sampling error concentrates; do not infer consistency solely from decreasing bias without controlling dispersion.

## Worked example

O004-W v1, hypothetical beta 1=-2, Cov(x, u)=3, Var(x)=6 with the note's regularity: plim=-2+3/6=-1.5. Population correlation contributes +0.5, so more data do not recover -2. Separately, an unbiased sequence with Var=4/n and epsilon 0.5 has error probability ≤(4/n)/(0.25)=16/n, tending0 (a bound may exceed1 for small n without becoming a probability claim). This is an application of the finished note's Chebyshev explanation, not a new theorem.

## Misconceptions and correction

Sign: “large samples cure endogeneity”, “unbiased implies consistent”, or treating covariance0 as conditional mean 0. Correction: the LLN retains Cov(x, u)/Var(x); concentration needs a correct limit, while finite-sample unbiasedness alone gives no shrinking-error guarantee. Hint1: identify numerator/denominator limits. Hint2: add their ratio to beta.
