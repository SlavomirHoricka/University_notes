## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

Compare same-sample simple/multiple regression output, calculate and interpret ordinary/adjusted R², and produce a short empirical report distinguishing conditional association, significance, fit, diagnostics and missing provenance. The question is what the available information lets us calculate or conclude, and which extra conditions the conclusion needs.

Use same dependent variable and observations for the prepared fit comparison. SSE denotes residual sum of squares here; SST=sum(y-ybar)²; k counts nonconstant regressors. With intercept $R^2=1-SSE/SST$; adjusted $R^2=1-(SSE/(n-k -1))/(SST/(n -1))$. R² describes sample variation around the mean accounted for by the fit, not the causal share or out-of-sample guarantee. CSV1000 rows has no units, sampling process or generating equation; u-column name alone gives no structural-error definition.

## Explanation steps

1. Define research question and choose transparent specification. Simple price model summarizes its bivariate association. Multiple model price slope holds income/gasoline fixed. Read an intercept cautiously at all regressors 0 if outside relevant support.
2. Report slope*one-unit change with units, held-fixed variables and sign. Control-induced slope changes are compatible with omitted-variable effects, not proof the richer model removes all omitted factors. A t statistic compares estimate with a stated null relative to its conventional se, conditional on maintained inference assumptions; it is not causal identification/economic importance.
3. Intercept OLS orthogonality yields total=fitted variation+SSE. Hence R²=1-SSE/SST. Adding regressors to the same sample retains the old fit by setting new coefficients 0; minimized SSE cannot increase, R² cannot decrease. Adjusted R² penalizes df; it can fall or be negative. Changes of sample/y invalidate this simple nesting argument.
4. Calculate adjusted fit using n -1 versus n-k -1. Never confuse explained sum of squares with SSE or count the intercept as a nonconstant regressor.
5. Structure the report: research question, known variables/units and sample/provenance, justified equation, readable coefficient+se+fit table, held-fixed interpretations and uncertainty, fit and diagnostic decisions, remaining assumptions and policy limits. Proposed income/private-transport or gasoline/substitution mechanisms are hypotheses, not identified results. Missing u definition prevents treating it as an ordinary structural control.
6. This lesson compares already-prepared output/calculations; hands-on R/other-software command competence and raw-data reruns are not assessed by this plan. No optimal fare claim follows without objective/cost/capacity/causal response.

## Misconceptions and correction

Sign: treating99%R²as99%causal explanation, assuming adjusted fit cannot fall, reading unknown units as percent/dollars, orcallingall controls as proof of exogeneity. Correction: sample fit is SSE relative to SST; the adjustment compares variation adjusted for degrees of freedom; held-fixed comparison still needs mean assumptions andprovenance. Hint1: labelSSE residual andwriteboth denominators. Hint2: separate sample fit, conditional association andcausal claim in three sentences.

Score the original response before help. One ambiguous error only suggests a tentative misconception; establish it only from explicit reasoning or repeated diagnostic signs. Explain the relevant prepared step and its consequence; never label the learner from confidence or a single slip.
