## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

More production is only one change within a network. Benefits from consumption, direct amenities and losses from environmental pressure travel along different paths, so one output measure cannot determine the whole welfare effect.

Terms and symbols are defined in the explanation below and exact cited sections. In network lessons P=production, C=consumption, U=utility, R=natural resources, ER=exhaustible resources, RR=renewable resources, h=harvest rate, y=yield/growth, W=waste, A=assimilative capacity. These are qualitative labels; units are supplied explicitly only in our hypothetical comparisons.

## Explanation steps

1. In the core figure firms are production P and households consumption C. Labour, savings, wages and rent/interest are labels in that core. K is conventionally capital but is not separately defined by the source. The solid/dashed legend below belongs to the full network figure; do not apply it retroactively to every line of the simpler core.
2. In the full network solid arrows carry materials/energy: natural resources R support P, P supplies C; solid paths from R, P and C join waste W.
3. Waste connects to assimilative capacity A and can feed back to resources. The sign of the capacity branch is conditional; detailed inequalities are O003.
4. Dashed arrows carry utility effects: C has a positive link to U; resources have positive amenity links to U; waste above capacity has a negative amenity path to U. U represents benefit/wellbeing without a numerical scale.
5. Plus/minus denote qualitative direction, not coefficients, euro values or a sum that permits net-welfare calculation. The lower-case r appears on a material return path but has no supplied definition, rate or equation. Identifying that visible path is supported; assigning a verified recycling parameter is not.

## Worked example

**O002-W1 v1, instruction only.** Agent-created scenario: production and consumption rise while waste exceeds the capacity shown by the diagram. Explain what can be traced without calculating net wellbeing.

**Worked solution:** Step 1: trace resources → P → C as material/energy. Step 2: outputs from economic activity join W, then the W>A branch feeds back negatively to resources and through a negative amenity path to U. Step 3: C still has a positive utility path and R a positive amenity path. Step 4: positive and negative directions coexist; no magnitude/utility function resolves their net effect. K has only a conventional interpretation; r remains undefined. This supports tracing mechanisms, not ranking the policy numerically.

## Misconceptions and correction

Sign: dashed arrows become shipments, or a positive arrow is treated as a numerical utility value. Tentative after one answer; use a fresh multi-path account before concluding. Correction: read the legend first, distinguish a physical path from a wellbeing effect, then ask what magnitude information is absent. Hint 1: identify the arrow type. Hint 2: compare the positive consumption and negative waste utility paths.

Score the original answer first; use at most the two hints above, then explain the prepared missing step. Corrective feedback creates exposure.

**O002-C1 v1, fresh correction, assistance allowed:** Repair this account: “P and C determine all wellbeing; waste stops at the sink; every plus is a euro gain.” Supply R→P→C, waste feedback when W>A, three utility paths, legend, and limitations on K/r.

**Key:** R→P→C physical flows; R/P/C feed W→A, W>A negatively affects resources and U. U has C positive, R amenity positive and excess-waste amenity negative paths, so P/C alone are insufficient. Solid materials/energy, dashed utility; signs qualitative, no euro/net formula. K conventional capital without explicit definition; r undefined return label.
