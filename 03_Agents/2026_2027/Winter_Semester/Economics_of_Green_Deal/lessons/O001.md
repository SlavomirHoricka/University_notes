## Motivation, intuition, and definitions

A forest can supply timber, enjoyment and systemic services. Asking only what it sells misses other ways it matters. A function describes a relationship, so the same site can have several functions.

Terms and symbols are defined in the explanation below and exact cited sections. In network lessons P=production, C=consumption, U=utility, R=natural resources, ER=exhaustible resources, RR=renewable resources, h=harvest rate, y=yield/growth, W=waste, A=assimilative capacity. These are qualitative labels; units are supplied explicitly only in our hypothetical comparisons.

## Explanation steps

1. Ask whether the environmental feature supplies an input or possibility for economic activity: this is a resource. Wood/biomass are degradable renewable resources; fossil fuels/minerals are degradable non-renewable/exhaustible resources. Sunshine, wind and waves are separately classified as non-degradable in this source; space is also a resource.
2. Ask whether the relationship concerns receiving undesirable outputs: this is the sink function, interpreted through assimilative capacity. A sink does not imply unlimited capacity.
3. Ask whether people value aesthetic, cultural or recreational services directly: these are demand services. Forest recreation differs from timber production even at the same site.
4. Ask whether the relationship concerns broad life-supporting systems: climate, the hydrologic cycle and soil production belong to the biospheric category.
5. Keep categories distinct by mechanism while allowing coexistence. The source supplies this taxonomy, not a general formal definition of degradability; do not replace its wording with another taxonomy.

## Worked example

**O001-W1 v1, instruction only.** An agent-created forest example combines timber, recreation, receipt of undesirable outputs, and the hydrologic cycle. Classify each and compare wood, fossil fuels and wind in the source taxonomy.

**Worked solution:** Step 1: timber supplies an input, so resource. Step 2: recreation gives direct enjoyment, so demand service. Step 3: receipt/assimilation of outputs is sink service, without an unlimited-capacity claim. Step 4: the hydrologic cycle is a biospheric life-supporting service. Wood is degradable renewable; fossil fuels are degradable exhaustible; wind is non-degradable in the slide. The same place supports several relationships; its identity does not force one exclusive category.

## Misconceptions and correction

Sign: all environmental benefits are called resources, or sunshine is silently recategorized. Treat one response as tentative; a repeated conflation across fresh tasks supports diagnosis. Correction: identify the relationship (input, waste reception, direct enjoyment, life support), then retain the slide’s labels. Hint 1: ask what role each feature plays. Hint 2: contrast timber with recreation at the same forest.

Score the original answer first; use at most the two hints above, then explain the prepared missing step. Corrective feedback creates exposure.

**O001-C1 v1, fresh correction, assistance allowed:** A learner says recreation and soil production are resources simply because nature provides them, and that wood and wind have the same source label. Correct both claims; also identify the timber and waste-reception functions and explain coexistence.

**Key:** In this taxonomy recreation is demand service and soil production biospheric life support; timber is resource and waste reception sink. Wood is degradable renewable; wind separately non-degradable. Nature can provide several distinct functions in one location; calling every service a resource erases the source distinction.
