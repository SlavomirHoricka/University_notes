# Student presentation contract

Apply this contract when composing or refreshing HTML lessons. This vault's owner validated the prototype and these preferences on 2026-10-19. They govern the derived course presentation, not the academic notes or evidence criteria.

## Reading structure

Start each lesson with a numbered, clickable outline before the introduction. Use the actual numbered section names, short useful descriptions and working anchors; omit a redundant Step column and Step labels. Follow with a clear introduction, bullet learning objectives and prerequisites, then an early bullet glossary of the lesson's important terms and notation. Give every subsequent section/subsection a visible number. Organize the complete teaching content into meaningful parts, with complete reasoning, assumptions, worked examples and interpretations. Do not repeat a compressed tutor script and then the full explanation as two competing lessons.

Write directly to the learner. Present the knowledge rather than narrating what a slide, lecture or presentation says. Retain meaningful attribution to an author's argument, historical dates, uncertainty, conflicting source details, formula conditions and scope limits. Separate contrasting concepts into short paragraphs, lists or suitable tables. Bold key terms selectively. Preserve complete explanations and mathematical derivations; layout changes never justify replacing them with summaries.

Keep version tuples, hashes, scoring procedures, tutor routing, scheduling and record machinery out of the student reading flow. Preserve their provenance in metadata. Move inline PDF/slide/page labels to an optional Sources disclosure; keep meaningful source and assumption limits available. Hiding an original block is not evidence that its academic substance is taught: the visible lesson must actually retain it.

## Exercises, hints and quizzes

Separate each situation from a numbered **Solve the following** task list. Preserve every requested component and the full checked key when reformatting prepared exercises. Keep solutions behind separate reveal controls. Use reasoning-guiding hints: prompt the learner to identify quantities, conditions, comparisons, regimes or missing evidence before calculating or drawing a conclusion. Avoid answer fragments, category names that solve the question, or merely replacing numbers in a supplied formula.

Include a short topic-specific offline quiz alongside written practice. Use four distinct options and exactly one unambiguous correct answer per question. Distractors should reflect credible mistakes in the same reasoning: reversed roles/signs, wrong regimes, denominators or units, omitted conditions, confusion of observation and causation, or treating a hypothetical as a result. Avoid absurd alternatives and generic test-taking trivia. A short quiz normally has three to five questions; choose coherent coverage rather than a quota. Freeze the complete key and a specific explanation for every option before rendering. Author complete mathematical expressions explicitly. Never discover formula fragments with a text replacement that can split a decimal, coefficient, product, radical or matrix relation; syntax success alone does not verify the displayed equation. Check every displayed equality and its relation to the surrounding sentence. Verify every option against checked content. Supplemental questions may practise an existing method but cannot add curriculum, substitute for named assessment items, or establish learner evidence.

Bundle predetermined keys, marking and feedback locally: no API, network connection or model is needed to check answers. Support unanswered/wrong/correct feedback, one answer per question, checking the whole quiz, clearing a stale result after changing an answer and starting again. Keep controls keyboard accessible and feedback announced. Local drafts and quiz results are study conveniences only.

## Navigation and appearance

Preserve Paper & Ink: warm paper, forest-green type/highlights, quiet rules, generous reading space and the original non-rounded callouts/navigation. The final non-rounded preference supersedes the earlier rounding annotations. Use restrained highlights for important ideas and results.

Provide a course hub link in addition to course contents. Each lesson in the sidebar has a separate accessible expand/collapse control revealing its numbered parts and subsections. The active lesson and reading section stay identifiable; anchor navigation is smooth and respects reduced-motion preferences. Compact layouts retain a usable navigation toggle. On the collection hub remove redundant generic introductions/Using these courses blocks. Course contents should have a short substantive course description, not generic claims about complete explanations.

## Review

Check the actual visible final content and full quiz bank against checked notes/lessons, including alternative meanings and each wrong-option diagnosis. Require independent conversion review and final browser evidence. Verify task coverage, all anchors, figures/math, keyboard operation, desktop/compact layout, empty/wrong/correct and reset behavior, and marking while the preview server is stopped or the browser is offline. Preserve old exports and stable external anchors on refresh.
