Introduction

In this lesson, derive matched purchase and sale hedge cash flows.

03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Long hedge: A futures purchase used against a later cash purchase exposure.
  • Short hedge: A futures sale used against a later cash sale exposure.
  • Effective price: Cash purchase cost or sale revenue after the matching futures gain/loss.
04

A purchase hedge, derived step by step

Suppose a buyer needs \(Nq\) units of the same underlying asset at maturity \(T\) and opens \(N\) matching long futures contracts at \(F_0\). Ignore transaction costs and interest on interim margin flows, as in the source's cash-flow arithmetic. By maturity the cumulative futures gain is \(Nq(F_T-F_0)\). Buying the asset spot costs \(NqS_T\). Net positive expenditure is therefore

\[E_T=NqS_T-Nq(F_T-F_0) =Nq\big[F_0+(S_T-F_T)\big] =Nq(F_0+B_T).\]

If convergence gives \(B_T=0\), net expenditure equals \(NqF_0\). A rise in the spot purchase price is offset by a futures gain; a fall reduces the spot cost but creates a futures loss. The hedge fixes the effective opening price under these matching assumptions.

The lecture instead writes signed cash flow with outflows negative:

\[(F_T-F_0)-S_T=-F_0-B_T=-F_0.\]

This is the same accounting with the opposite sign for expenditure. Defining the sign first avoids interpreting the negative amount as a negative purchase price.

The handout's five-day example ends at a spot and futures price of 900, after opening at 1,000. The long has lost 100 on futures, so buying spot for 900 costs 1,000 after that loss. This illustrates the hedge even though the futures leg itself is unprofitable.

05

A sale hedge and early termination

A seller expecting to have the matching asset opens a short. Its futures gain is \(Nq(F_0-F_t)\), while its spot sale produces \(NqS_t\). The net receipt at time t is

\[R_t=NqS_t+Nq(F_0-F_t)=Nq(F_0+B_t).\]

The long buyer's positive expenditure at the same time is also \(Nq(F_0+B_t)\), while its signed cash flow is \(-Nq(F_0+B_t)\). At maturity the source's zero-basis condition fixes both purchase expenditure and sale revenue at the opening futures price.

Before maturity, \(B_t\) is unknown when the hedge is opened. This is basis risk: the uncertainty about the future spot–futures difference remains after the broad price movement has been hedged.

The handout says hedging is “possible only” on delivery dates, but its following formula explicitly describes an early hedge with remaining basis. Its operational distinction is between the exact opening-price lock at zero basis and the imperfect early hedge exposed to basis risk. The early net-cost formula exists; it does not guarantee a predetermined cost.

The handout's final early-hedge line writes \(-F_0+(F_t-S_t)\) but labels it “futures price − basis”. With its own definition \(B_t=S_t-F_t\), the displayed expression is \(-F_0-B_t\): the long buyer's signed cash flow. Its positive expenditure is \(F_0+B_t\). The verbal label therefore has an apparent sign inconsistency; the distinction between signed cash flow and positive expenditure above preserves the algebra.

06

What to carry forward

A futures trading gain is only one leg of a hedge. Combining it with the matching spot transaction gives the opening price plus the remaining basis.

At zero basis the hedge fixes the price; at an earlier date it leaves basis risk.

The result depends on matching quantity, asset and maturity and on the source's cash-flow assumptions. Margins and marking to market explains why funding obligations may still arise before the hedge reaches its final result.

07

Connect the reasoning

  1. Buyer: futures gain \(X(F_t-F_0)\); spot purchase costs \(X S_t\). \(E=X S_t-X(F_t-F_0)=X[F_0+(S_t-F_t)]=X(F_0+B)\). \(\mathrm{CF}=-E\).
  2. Seller: spot revenue \(X S_t\) plus short gain \(X(F_0-F_t)\). \(R=X S_t+X(F_0-F_t)=X(F_0+B)\). The same expression has a different economic label; do not swap buyer expenditure and seller inflow signs.
  3. At matching maturity conditional convergence \(B_T=0\) gives \(E=R=X F_0\) and \(\mathrm{CF}=-X F_0\). Futures loss on a falling market is offset by cheaper purchase; futures loss on rising market by higher sale price for seller.
  4. Before maturity the closing basis is unknown at inception. The early hedge still operates but leaves buyer expenditure \(E=XF_0+XB_t\) or seller receipt \(R=XF_0+XB_t\), rather than a guaranteed opening-price lock. This is basis risk, not the same as absolute spot movement.
  5. The notes flag the source verbal early-hedge sign error: \(-F_0+(F_t-S_t)=-F_0-B\), buyer signed flow. Positive expenditure is \(F_0+B\). Never reuse the mistaken label.
  6. Zero-basis effective-price accounting does not remove interim calls. Daily settlement requires funding and the source ignores its interest; a final hedge outcome is not proof of no cash demand.
08

Worked example

Hypothetical buyer/seller: \(X=100\) units, \(F_0=50\); close early with \(F_t=54\), \(S_t=55\), \(B=1\).

Buyer long gain \(400\), spot cost \(5500\), \(E=5100\),\(\mathrm{CF}=-5100\). Seller short loss \(-400\), spot sale \(5500\),\(R=5100\).

Same effective \(51\)/unit, not guaranteed opening \(50\) because \(B=1\). If matching maturity instead \(F_t=S_t=55\), buyer gain \(500\) and seller loss \(-500\);

each positive cost/revenue \(5000\) (\(50\)/unit), buyer signed \(-5000\). Source handout long opened \(1000\) and ends \(F_t=S_t=900\) loses \(100\) but buys spot \(900\), effective \(1000\).

Calls and flow interest excluded from this simple cash-flow sum.

10.1 Question 1

Buy 100 units at spot 55 after a long hedge opened at 50 and closed at 54. What is net purchase cost?

Reveal explanation

Spot cost 5,500 minus long gain 400 equals 5,100; remaining basis is 1 per unit.

10.2 Question 2

A seller uses the matching short hedge in the same early-close example. What is effective revenue?

Reveal explanation

Spot receipts 5,500 plus short P/L −400 give positive revenue 5,100.

10.3 Question 3

When does this simple matching hedge recover the opening futures price exactly per unit?

Reveal explanation

The cash/futures cancellation leaves F0 plus terminal basis, which equals F0 only when the basis vanishes.
