02

Introduction

In this lesson, separate added-position margin from net and intraday funding.

2.1 What you’ll learn

  • When a member adds contracts during a day compute existing and new cohorts from their respective reference prices, total daily settlement, incremental and final initial-margin requirement, end-of-day net funding, and conditional temporary funding before settlement. Assume dollars-per-contract \(q=1\), beginning balance exactly the old initial requirement, no withdrawal and all longs in the same specified contract.

2.2 Before you begin

03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Position cohort: Contracts sharing the same opening or previous-settlement price basis.
  • Incremental initial margin: Extra required margin from the newly added contracts.
  • Intraday funding: Cash needed before later settlement proceeds become available.
04

Problem 5: clearing a larger long position

The member begins with 85 long contracts settled at $42,500 and initial margin $2,000 per contract. It adds 15 long contracts at $43,750. All contracts end the new day at $43,000. Existing contracts gain

\[85(43000-42500)=\$42,500,\]

while new contracts lose

\[15(43000-43750)=-\$11,250.\]

Net daily settlement is +$31,250. The 15 new positions require \(15\times2000=\$30,000\) additional initial margin. If the initial account holds exactly \(85\times2000=\$170,000\), the new requirement for 100 contracts is \(200,000\), while after settlement the account has $201,250. On an end-of-day net basis, no additional net cash is needed; $1,250 exceeds the new initial requirement.

The required incremental initial margin is still $30,000. If it must be posted when the new positions are entered, before the day's $31,250 credit is available, it requires $30,000 of temporary funding.

The PDF asks for total including initial margin but does not specify intraday sequencing, beginning excess balance or withdrawal rules. The answer therefore distinguishes the new margin obligation from net end-of-day funding rather than assuming their timing is identical.

05

Daily profit and loss, with units

Let \(F_0\) be the opening futures price, \(F_t\) the settlement price at the end of day t, \(N\) the number of contracts and \(q\) the multiplier converting one quoted price unit into money per contract. With a constant position size and multiplier,

\[G_t^{\mathrm{long}}=Nq(F_t-F_{t-1}),\qquad G_t^{\mathrm{short}}=Nq(F_{t-1}-F_t)=-G_t^{\mathrm{long}}.\]

A price rise credits the long and debits the short; a fall reverses these transfers. The lecture writes the price difference for a normalized unit; \(Nq\) restores the contract units used by the seminar's tick examples. For a commodity quoted in dollars per pound, \(q\) is pounds per contract. For a contract quoted directly in dollars per contract, \(q=1\).

Adding the daily transfers cancels every intermediate price:

\[\sum_{t=1}^{k}G_t^{\mathrm{long}} =Nq\big[(F_1-F_0)+(F_2-F_1)+\cdots +(F_k-F_{k-1})\big] =Nq(F_k-F_0).\]

The short obtains the opposite amount. This telescoping sum explains why the total trading result equals the closing minus opening price, even though it is paid in installments. The account can require cash along the way even when the final cumulative result is favorable. The source's reasoning ignores interest earned or paid on the timing of settlement cash flows.

06

The three margin concepts

Initial margin, \(I\), is the required deposit when opening a position. Both the long and short must post it.

The lecture says it is the trader's money in the margin account, not the price paid for the underlying asset. Because it is only a fraction of the underlying contract value, it creates leverage: a given movement in the full contract's price can be large relative to this initial deposit.

The seminar gives 5–15% as a usual illustrative range rather than a universal required rate.

Maintenance margin, \(M\), is the minimum allowed account balance. Daily losses may reduce the balance below \(M\). A margin call is then a notice to restore the balance to the specified target. The lecture allows a target of maintenance or initial margin; the handout describes restoring maintenance; the seminar's formula uses initial margin. The target must therefore be stated when doing calculations.

The lecture and handout call the extra deposited funds variation margin. The seminar also uses that phrase for the daily transfer of settlement gains/losses and writes “\(\text{Variation Margin}=\text{Initial Margin}-\text{Margin balance}\).” That expression describes a top-up to initial margin when the balance is deficient;

it is not a formula saying every daily settlement payment equals \(I\) minus the balance. These meanings share the aim of limiting unpaid losses, but use different cash-account conventions.

Below, “daily gain/loss” means the price-based transfer and “top-up” means the additional deposit after a call.

The seminar describes maintenance both as a minimum balance and as a payment made after adverse movement. The minimum is the threshold; the required payment is the amount needed to restore the chosen target. Keeping these quantities separate prevents confusing a $2,000 threshold with a $2,000 deposit in every case.

07

Connect the reasoning

  1. Separate cohorts because the new contracts did not experience the entire prior-to-current price movement.
  2. Compute signed \(G\) for each, then sum \(G_{\mathrm{total}}\); one cohort may lose even when net gains.
  3. Compute incremental new margin \(n_{\mathrm{new}}m\) independently of \(\mathrm{P/L}\). This obligation exists even if settlement later funds it.
  4. Under end-of-day netting, available \(A_{\mathrm{beforefunding}}=n_{\mathrm{old}}m+G_{\mathrm{total}}\). Additional net \(\text{cash}=\max(0,\text{new requirement}-\text{available})\); \(\text{surplus}=\max(0,\text{available}-\text{requirement})\). Surplus is not total \(\mathrm{P/L}\).
  5. If incremental margin must be posted at entry before settlement, \(n_{\mathrm{new}}m\) temporary funding is needed under the assumed exact old balance. The resulting later surplus includes that deposit; do not count it as trade profit.
  6. With unspecified intraday sequence, present conditional net versus entry funding rather than one universal amount. This is account arithmetic, not a general broker policy.
08

Worked example

Source case: \(85\) old longs,\(F_{\mathrm{old}}=42500\),\(15\) new at \(43750\),\(F_{\mathrm{end}}=43000\),\(m=2000\). Old \(G=85\times 500=42500\);

new \(G=15\times (-750)=-11250\); total \(31250\).

Increment \(30000\); final requirement \(200000\);

beginning plus settlement: \(170000+31250=201250\). End-of-day extra net cash \(0\), surplus \(1250\).

If entry precedes settlement and new initial must be posted then, temporary funding \(30000\). If it is deposited, ending account \(231250\) and excess \(31250\) over requirement;

trading gain remains \(31250\) and deposits total \(200000\). Decision: old and new price bases differ;

positive settlement does not erase the obligation or timing.

10

Check your reasoning: an offline quiz

Choose one answer per question. Check individual answers or check the whole quiz. Each option includes feedback on the reasoning, and everything works offline.

10.1 Question 1

85 old longs gain 500 each; 15 new longs lose 750 each. What is total settlement P/L?

Reveal explanation

Compute cohorts separately: \(85\times 500-15\times 750=31,250\).

10.2 Question 2

15 added contracts require 2,000 initial margin each. What is incremental requirement?

Reveal explanation

Only the newly added 15 contracts determine the incremental requirement; final total requirement is different.

10.3 Question 3

Beginning margin 170,000 plus settlement 31,250 exceeds final requirement 200,000. What remains possible?

Reveal explanation

Net closing sufficiency does not establish the timing of intraday funding availability.

Check your understanding

Avoid these mistakes: treating new contracts as held since yesterday; adding margin to profit; equating net funding with entry obligation. Correction: label each cohort's entry/reference, compute \(G\) independently, then compare cash with requirement at the specified time.
