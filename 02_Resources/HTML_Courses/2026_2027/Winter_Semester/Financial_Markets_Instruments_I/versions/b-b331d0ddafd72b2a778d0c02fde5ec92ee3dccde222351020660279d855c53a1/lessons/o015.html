03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Calendar spread: The specified difference between earlier and later delivery quotes.
  • Forward loan: Funding locked today but drawn at the earlier delivery date.
  • Carry interval: The interval between the two deliveries, rather than time from today to the later date.
04

Carry between two delivery dates

Let \(F_0^1,F_0^2\) be current prices for deliveries at \(T^1<T^2\), both measured in years, and put \(\Delta T=T^2-T^1\). Today go long the earlier contract, short the later one, and arrange a forward loan for the interval \([T^1,T^2]\).

The source assigns zero initial cash to each agreement. At \(T^1\) draw \(F_0^1\) under the arranged loan and pay the earlier contract to acquire the asset.

Hold it only for \(\Delta T\); the source measures income and financing cost relative to the amount \(F_0^1\) financed.

At \(T^2\) deliver for \(F_0^2\) and repay the loan:

\[0=[F_0^2+dF_0^1\Delta T]-[F_0^1+rF_0^1\Delta T].\]

Moving the delivery-price terms to one side gives

\[F_0^1-F_0^2=F_0^1(d-r)(T^2-T^1),\qquad F_0^2=F_0^1[1+(r-d)\Delta T].\]

The earlier-minus-later spread equals carry over the intervening interval. Using \(T^2\) instead of \(T^2-T^1\) would charge for time before the asset was acquired. Using \(S_0\) rather than \(F_0^1\) would change the financing base in the source's construction. This is a separate interval model with funding terms locked today, not a license to combine inconsistent rates across maturities.

05

What makes the replication valid

The lecture's linear rates, known income/costs, tradeable underlying, available short sales, matching delivery and locked funding underpin the derivation. A convenience benefit from physical ownership need not be a cash coupon transferable to a short seller.

Thus listing convenience yield on slide 2 does not establish that every asset supports both exact arbitrages. External qualification: Damodaran's NYU “Futures Arbitrage,” sections “Storable Commodities” and “Stock Index Futures,” explains that funding differences, shorting costs and uncertain dividends weaken exact replication. These are reasons to inspect feasibility before applying the equation. Damodaran, consulted sections.

The source aggregates all futures flows at delivery. Actual futures have daily variation margin, as explained in the Week 1 margin note.

That cash timing, funding and reinvestment can matter; this note preserves the lecture's simplified replication rather than treating a forward-style cash-flow table as a complete margin-funding model.

The simple formula also differs from compounded conventions: do not replace it silently by an exponential.

06

Worked example

Prompt: Two current delivery prices concern one matching asset at \(T^1=0.5\) and \(T^2=1\) years. The earlier quote is \(F_0^1=100\).

A forward loan can be locked today for the intervening interval at annual simple \(r=0.06\); known holding income is \(d=0.02\) on the source model base \(F_0^1\).

All trades are reversible and source terminal/timing assumptions hold. Draw the dated zero-investment package, derive and compute fair \(F_0^2\) and earlier-minus-later spread, and interpret its sign.

With later quote \(103.000000\), compute package profit and its direction. Explain why \(S_0\) and \(T^2\) cannot replace the financing base and interval, and why this separate interval construction need not follow by mixing unrelated spot carry rates.

Solution: Today long earlier delivery, short later delivery and arrange a forward loan; each initial flow is zero.

At \(T^1\) draw \(100\) and pay it for the asset. At \(T^2\) receive \(F_0^2+dF_0^1\Delta T\) and repay \(F_0^1(1+r\Delta T)\).

Thus \(0=F_0^2+dF_0^1\Delta T-F_0^1-rF_0^1\Delta T\), where \(\Delta T=0.5\). Hence \(F_0^2=F_0^1[1+(r-d)\Delta T]=102.000000\), and \(F_0^1-F_0^2=F_0^1(d-r)\Delta T=-2.000000\).

The later delivery is dearer; the defined earlier-minus-later spread is negative.

At the high later quote use that same long-earlier/short-later package; terminal profit is \(103.000000+0.02(100)(0.5)-100-0.06(100)(0.5)=1.000000\).

Its reverse changes the sign. The asset is acquired only at \(T^1\) for \(F_0^1\), so \(S_0\) is not the loan principal and \(T^2\) includes time before ownership.

Funding and income must be locked and compatible for this interval. Combining different conventions or uncontracted future rates would not establish the same riskless package;

margin cash timing and trading restrictions remain qualifications.

Check your reasoning: an offline quiz

Choose one answer per question. Check individual answers or check the whole quiz. Each option includes feedback on the reasoning, and everything works offline.

8.1 Question 1

Earlier future 100, deliveries at 0.5 and 1 years, \(r=6\%\), \(d=2\%\). What is fair later delivery in the interval model?

Reveal explanation

Finance the earlier purchase 100 for the intervening 0.5 years at net carry 4%.

8.2 Question 2

What is the earlier-minus-later spread at that fair quote?

Reveal explanation

The defined order is 100−102; units are price units, not a percentage.

8.3 Question 3

At later quote 103, which interval package earns 1 before stated frictions?

Reveal explanation

Acquire at 100 at the earlier date, receive 103 plus income 1 later and repay 103: net 1. The separate interval funding convention matters.
