Introduction

03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Deferred-payment value: The lesson’s discounted value of an assumed maturity-paid price difference.
  • Remaining term: The time between valuation and the assumed payment date.
  • Daily settlement: Actual transfer of futures price changes along the path.
04

The lecture's value convention

The lecture labels \(S_T-F_0\) for a normalized long and \(F_0-S_T\) for a short as value at expiration. Those expressions equal the cumulative payoff described in the handout. Before expiration it presents a deferred-payment illustration:

\[V_t^{\mathrm{long}}=\frac{F_t-F_0}{1+r(T-t)},\qquad V_t^{\mathrm{short}}=\frac{F_0-F_t}{1+r(T-t)}.\]

Here \(r\) is the simple interest rate and \(T-t\) the remaining time in matching units. The denominator discounts a cash difference assumed paid at \(T\) back to t. At inception \(F_t=F_0\), so the source's value is zero.

Keep this illustration distinct from the lecture's actual daily marking-to-market mechanism. Slide 6 realizes changes day by day;

slide 11 describes a price difference as income at maturity. The selected sources do not reconcile the timing conventions or provide a valuation of interest on all daily settlement flows.

The discounted expression should therefore be learned as slide 11's stated deferred-payment representation, without treating it as the margin-account balance or as a proved universal value of a daily-settled contract.

05

Connect the reasoning

  1. Identify numerator as the price difference for a normalized unit, then multiply by stated \(X=Nq\) when using full-contract money.
  2. Use annual \(r\) as a decimal with remaining years, or another consistently matched time unit. Denominator \(1+r\Delta t\) is dimensionless; \(V\) has money units after multiplying \(X\).
  3. The assumed maturity cash difference equals today's representation times \(1+r\Delta t\); divide to move backward under simple-interest assumption. This reasoning depends on payment being deferred, not paid daily.
  4. Long/short values are opposite. At inception \(F_t=F_0\) gives \(0\); at maturity \(\Delta t=0\) and, under convergence, \(F_t=S_t\), \(V_{\mathrm{long}}=X(S_t-F_0)\), short opposite.
  5. An actual margin account also contains deposits, withdrawals and daily realized transfers. Its balance cannot be inferred from this formula alone. Nor does this illustration value interest on every realized daily transfer. Do not call a negative \(V\) negative margin or a loss cap.
06

Worked example

Hypothetical \(X=100\), \(F_0=50\), \(F_t=54\), \(r=0.10/\text{year}\), \(\Delta t=0.5\text{ year}\). Maturity-assumed price difference is \(400\);

denominator \(1.05\); lecture \(V_{\mathrm{long}}=400/1.05=\$380.952381\),short -$380.952381.

At inception numerator \(0\to 0\). At matching maturity \(S_t=54\),time \(0\)\(\to\)long \(400\),short \(-400\).

If the real futures gains were paid daily, \(400\) is cumulative undiscounted trade gain ignoring interest; \(V=380.95\) is not the account balance, which needs deposits/withdrawals/path. The source supplies no universal reconciliation.

Check your reasoning: an offline quiz

Choose one answer per question. Check individual answers or check the whole quiz. Each option includes feedback on the reasoning, and everything works offline.

8.1 Question 1

For 100 units, price difference 54−50, simple annual discount rate 10% and remaining half-year, what is deferred-payment long value?

Reveal explanation

Discount the 400 maturity-assumed difference by \(1+0.10\times 0.5=1.05\).

8.2 Question 2

What is the corresponding short value under the same model?

Reveal explanation

Reverse the sign while preserving the model’s common discounting assumptions.

8.3 Question 3

Can this deferred value alone determine a daily-settled margin account?

Reveal explanation

The lecture’s deferred-payment convention is not a universal daily-account valuation identity.
