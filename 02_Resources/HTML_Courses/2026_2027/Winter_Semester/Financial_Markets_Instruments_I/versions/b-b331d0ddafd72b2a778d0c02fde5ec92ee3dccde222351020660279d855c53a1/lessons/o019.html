03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Tracking portfolio: A portfolio matching the index exposure and contract scaling.
  • Dividend yield: The specified cash-income yield used in the carry convention.
  • Spot cancellation: Opposing terminal spot terms disappear when hedge quantities and dates match.
04

Stock-index replication and cancellation of spot risk

Let \(S_0\) be today's value of a portfolio matching the index, \(S_1\) its delivery-date value, \(F_0\) the futures price in matching portfolio units, \(r\) the annual simple borrowing rate, \(d\) annual dividend yield and \(T\) years. Borrow \(S_0\), buy the tracking portfolio and short matching index futures.

At delivery sell the portfolio for \(S_1\), receive cumulative futures gain \(F_0-S_1\), collect dividends and repay funding. If dividends over the interval are modeled as \(dTS_0\), the net payoff is

\[\Pi=S_1-S_0(1+rT)+dTS_0+(F_0-S_1).\]

The uncertain \(S_1\) cancels: a lower portfolio resale price produces a larger short-futures gain. Setting remaining cash to zero gives

\[F_0=S_0+(r-d)TS_0.\]

A contract multiplier must be applied consistently to portfolio value and futures gains; mixing an index-point quotation with an unscaled money portfolio invalidates the cancellation. Actual variation margin is daily, while the source aggregates it into a terminal balance.

05

The source's terminal-futures-payoff typo

Slide 10's table correctly gives the short-futures balance as \(F_0-S_1\), and the final fair-price formula is \(F_0=S_0+(r-d)TS_0\). Its displayed intermediate equation, however, writes the last parenthesis as \(F_0-S_0\).

That subtracts today's spot value instead of the terminal spot value, leaving the uncertain \(S_1\) uncancelled and failing to produce the stated replication. The consistent parenthesis is \(F_0-S_1\), as used in the derivation above.

The dividend term in the source equation is \(dTS_0\); it includes the time factor.

This note preserves the source typo explicitly rather than using it as a valid algebra step.

06

Implementation limits and priority

The source identifies costs of forming and maintaining the tracking portfolio, dividend taxation and uncertain dividend timing as market imperfections. An ETF can reduce the practical cost of acquiring a portfolio that copies an index, but the source does not establish zero tracking cost or certain dividends for every ETF.

The core concept is replication and cancellation; ETF implementation is supporting detail whose purpose is to show why an ideal equality may be costly to trade.

Bounded external verification: Damodaran's NYU “Futures Arbitrage,” “Stock Index Futures” assumptions, likewise requires known dividends and compatible funding/shorting. This corroborates the limits rather than overriding the course's simple-rate convention. Consulted passage. No current contract terms or live ETF recommendation are inferred.

Worked example

Prompt: An exactly tracking hypothetical index portfolio is quoted at \(S_0=1000\) index points. A matching future has monetary multiplier 10 currency units/point, annual simple funding \(r=0.06\), known dividend yield \(d=0.02\), and \(T=0.5\) years to delivery.

Derive fair futures points by borrowing, buying the portfolio and shorting the future. At terminal spot \(S_1=1100\) and a quoted future \(1023.000000\) points, list the scaled portfolio resale, dividends, cumulative futures payoff and loan repayment and calculate net modeled profit.

Explain the cancellation, diagnose the source intermediate \(F_0-S_0\) typo, and identify ETF/tracking/tax/dividend-timing/daily-margin limitations. No fees/taxes are included in the hypothetical arithmetic.

Solution: Borrow \(10000.000000\) currency units and buy the matching scaled portfolio; the future has zero idealized opening value, so net opening cash is zero.

At delivery receive \(mS_1=11000.000000\), dividends \(mdTS_0=100.000000\) and short-futures cash \(m(F_{\mathrm{quote}}-S_1)=-770.000000\); repay \(mS_0(1+rT)=10300.000000\).

Total is \(m[S_1-S_0(1+rT)+dTS_0+(F_{\mathrm{quote}}-S_1)]=30.000000\). The two \(S_1\) terms cancel under exact tracking and matching units.

At zero profit this gives \(F_0=S_0[1+(r-d)T]=1020.000000\) points. Source slide10 has the correct table/final formula but its intermediate last parenthesis \(F_0-S_0\) fails to cancel terminal \(S_1\);

the consistent short payoff is \(F_0-S_1\). Its dividend term already includes \(T\).

An ETF may reduce portfolio formation costs; it does not establish perfect/zero-cost tracking or certain dividends.

Taxes, uncertain timing, funding/shorting restrictions and daily variation-margin cash dates can defeat this ideal terminal replication. The profit here is conditional on the stated implementability assumptions, not a live trading claim.

9.1 Question 1

Index 1000, simple \(r=6\%\), \(d=2\%\), half-year. What is fair index future?

Reveal explanation

Apply S0[1+(r−d)T] under the stated simple convention.

9.2 Question 2

Which short-futures payoff cancels the resale value S1 in the terminal replication?

Reveal explanation

Add S1 from portfolio resale to F0−S1. The checked source typo F0−S0 leaves uncertain S1 uncancelled.

9.3 Question 3

Multiplier 10, quoted future 1023 versus fair 1020, exactly matching ideal cash flows. What is net package profit?

Reveal explanation

The three-point mispricing is multiplied by 10; the result remains conditional on tracking, dividends and funding assumptions.
