03

Motivation and explanation

An index quote has no money scale until multiplied by the contract multiplier. A minimum tick is a permitted quote increment; tick value is multiplier times that increment. For fixed long \(N\), \(G=N\times\text{multiplier}\times(F_{\mathrm{end}}-F_{\mathrm{start}})\); reverse the sign for a short. Closing ends the position; holding instead substitutes EDSP as the final endpoint. Do not add the whole held payoff to a closed payoff. Margin return divides trading \(G\) by the stated initial collateral, whereas contract exposure follows the multiplier and index level. Simple annualisation multiplies that fraction by \(365/\text{elapsed days}\); it magnifies short-horizon leverage and is not a prediction. EDSP is fixed by the contract's observation/trim rule: in the finished note's hypothetical example a sample of observations is taken and extremes removed. The specified benchmark is distinct from an arbitrary quote. Real contract terms require verification outside this prepared illustration.

Read the contract before calculating

A quotation alone has no monetary meaning until the unit of trading and minimum price increment are known. Let \(N\) be the number of contracts, \(\delta\) the tick size in quotation units and \(v\) the money value of one tick. The change from opening quotation \(F_0\) to closing quotation \(F_t\) is \((F_t-F_0)/\delta\) ticks. Thus, before fees and financing effects,

\[\Pi_{\mathrm{long}}=N\frac{F_t-F_0}{\delta}v, \qquad \Pi_{\mathrm{short}}=-\Pi_{\mathrm{long}}.\]

This is a unit conversion: quotation change divided by quotation units per tick, multiplied by currency per tick. The long benefits when the quotation rises. The short benefits when it falls. The lecture's index and currency calculations use the same rule, even though their quoted units and settlement currencies differ. L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 4 L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 6

Margins and marking to market explains why daily transfers telescope to the opening-to-closing difference. Initial margin is a deposit supporting the position, rather than the value of the underlying asset. The tutorial return convention is

\[R_{\mathrm{period}}=\frac{\Pi}{N M_0},\qquad R_{\mathrm{annual,simple}}=R_{\mathrm{period}}\frac{365}{d},\]

where \(M_0\) is initial margin per contract and \(d\) is the holding period in days. This convention explains the large quoted returns: the denominator is margin, not the contract's full exposure. The annual figure linearly rescales the particular trade; it does not establish a repeatable annual investment outcome. In calculations using this convention, subsequent margin calls, withdrawals, fees and financing are outside the displayed denominator. SIFT 500: index points become euros

The tutorial Stock Index Futures Tutorial 500 (SIFT 500) uses an index composed of share prices of the 500 largest companies by market capitalization. Its multiplier is EUR 10 per index point. A quote of 5,433 therefore represents EUR 54,330 of contract value. One tick is half an index point, worth \(0.5\times10=\text{EUR }5\); two ticks are one point. Initial margin is EUR 1,000, about 1.8% of EUR 54,330 in the source's example. L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 3

Tutorial specifications and footnotes, including the multiplier and final settlement averaging rule. L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 3

Delivery months are March, June, September and December. The last trading day is the last business day of that month, at 10:30 a.m.; delivery is the next business day. The exchange delivery settlement price (EDSP) averages underlying-index observations every 15 seconds from 10:10 through 10:30 a.m., discarding the 12 highest and 12 lowest values. This procedure aims to smooth excessive fluctuations shortly before maturity. It defines a final settlement benchmark rather than simply using the final observed index value. L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 3

Closing after five days

The long buys 20 contracts at \(F_0=5825.0\) and sells 20 matching contracts five days later at \(F_5=5812.5\). The quotation falls by 12.5 index points, equal to \(-12.5/0.5=-25\) ticks. Therefore,

\[\Pi=20(-25)(5)=-\text{EUR }2{,}500.\]

The return on opening margin is \(-2500/(20\times1000)=-12.5\%\). Simple annual scaling gives \(-0.125\times365/5=-9.125\), approximately the source's displayed \(-912\%\). A scaled return below \(-100\%\) reflects the short holding period and the chosen margin denominator; the observed five-day loss itself remains EUR 2,500. L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 4

Holding until cash settlement

Delivering every constituent share in the index's weights would create extremely high transaction costs, so the tutorial index contract settles in cash. If the position remains open, the final marking-to-market transfer compares EDSP with the previous day's closing futures quotation. The source gives EDSP \(5810.0\) and an additional loss of \(20(-5)(5)=-\text{EUR }500\). Its arithmetic treats \(5812.5\) as that preceding settlement quotation. This is an alternative to the five-day closing trade, not an additional payment owed after that trade has already closed the position. With the same opening quotation, the total hold-to-settlement loss is \(20(5810-5825)10=-\text{EUR }3{,}000\) (derived from the source's quotations). L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 4

The source timeline displays the closing quotation and final EDSP. Use the closing-out path and the maturity path as alternatives. L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 4

05

Worked example

O009-W1 / item_version 1, worked_example. Prompt: Hypothetical SIFT-style index contract: multiplier €10 per index point, minimum tick 0.5 point, initial margin €1000 per contract. A long 20-contract position opens at 5825; it could close at 5812.5 after 5 days, or instead remain open to cash EDSP 5810. Compute tick value, closing signed \(\mathrm{P/L}\) and percentage return on initial margin, simple annualised closing return using 365 days, and the separate held-to-settlement total. Explain why these are alternative paths, what EDSP does, and whether the return is an index investment yield or repeatable annual forecast. Interpret the multiplier/margin and explain the meaning of a trimmed-sample EDSP, without asserting current exchange terms.

Steps and interpretation: \(\text{One tick}=\text{€}10/\text{point}\times0.5\text{ point}=\text{€}5\) per contract. Closing \(G=20\times 10\times (5812.5-5825)=\text{€}-2500\); initial deposited margin €20000, so return -12.5%, annualised -912.5% by simple scaling. Held \(G=20\times 10\times (5810-5825)=\text{€}-3000\); this alternative includes the whole opening-to-EDSP move, not the already-closed trade plus settlement. A cash-settled contract pays a difference using EDSP, not delivery of index stocks. EDSP is a rule-defined sample benchmark (the finished-note example trims extreme samples), not any chosen last quote. Multiplier scales exposure; margin is collateral, not index notional. Margin leverage/simple annualisation does not establish an underlying yield or a repeatable forecast. These given specification values are pedagogical, not current market terms.

Misconceptions and correction

An answer adding two terminal payoffs suggests endpoint confusion. Draw a fork at opening: one branch closes, the other holds. A very large annualised figure can be arithmetically correct but its base is collateral and its repeated-return assumption unjustified. Observe the learner's reasoning before diagnosing: a sign or omitted word alone is tentative. Score before help. O009-C1 / item_version 1, narrow_correction. Fresh prompt: Fresh narrow check: long \(2\), multiplier \(10\), opening \(100\), early close \(101\) or instead EDSP \(99\). Is total gain \(0\)? Explain. Key and rubric: No. Closed branch \(+20\); separately held branch \(-20\). They are alternatives, not additive outcomes. Pass only if the keyed repaired component is supplied; partial for an omission or arithmetic slip; fail for the same conflicting mechanism. C1 is not a full independent check. After two ineffective hints show the relevant worked step; after another failed correction pause and return to the named prerequisite or explanation.
