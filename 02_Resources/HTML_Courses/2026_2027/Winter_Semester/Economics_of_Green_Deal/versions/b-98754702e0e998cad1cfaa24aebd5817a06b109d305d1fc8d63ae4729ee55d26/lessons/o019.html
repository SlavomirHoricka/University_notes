03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Scenario projection: A conditional model outcome under a specified pathway and assumptions.
  • Mitigation: Reducing the drivers of climate change.
  • Adaptation: Managing exposure and vulnerability to impacts that remain.
04

Climate scenarios, policy and adaptation

A climate scenario asks what follows if a coherent set of social, technological, emissions and policy conditions occurs. It is not an assigned probability or an unconditional forecast. Use older RCP projections alongside newer SSP-labelled examples; the two naming systems and their temperature baselines must be distinguished.

This map follows pp. 40–45 and 43's paired emissions/concentration graphic; it explains why the policy discussion needs both mitigation and adaptation.

4.1 SSP assumptions and RCP forcing pathways

The source presents SSP2, “middle of the road,” as its baseline socioeconomic narrative, extending major development trends without a large assumed policy/technology break. That is the lecture's baseline choice, not a universal definition that SSP2 equals one forcing outcome.

Shared Socioeconomic Pathways describe demographic, economic and technological narratives; Representative Concentration Pathways describe emissions/concentration/forcing trajectories.

Radiative forcing measures a change in Earth's energy balance in W/m², as the embedded comments on pp. 41–42 explain. RCP labels 2.6/4.5/6.0/8.5 refer to approximate 2100 forcing levels, with different trajectories toward them.

The source associates 2.6/4.5/6.0 with approximately 490/650/850 ppm CO2-equivalent and leaves 8.5 unfinished. These are the slide's older shorthand values, not concentrations of CO2 alone;

the exact trajectory and whether a quoted value is a peak or endpoint matter. Its claim that forcing is “declining” should not be read as monotonic decline throughout every RCP.

There is a source inconsistency: p. 41 calls RCP6.0 “close to SSP2,” whereas p. 42 calls RCP8.5 “close to SSP2.” A socioeconomic family does not uniquely identify a forcing path. Authoritative clarification: IPCC AR6 WGI SPM BoxSPM.1 and note 22 define SSPx-y as socioeconomic family x plus approximate 2100 forcing y. The supplied p. 43 itself shows SSP2-4.5 as intermediate, alongside SSP1-1.9/SSP1-2.6 and SSP3-7.0/SSP5-8.5. IPCC AR6 WGI SPM, BoxSPM.1/note22.

Thus neither conflicting “close to” statement is used as a one-to-one mapping.

Entries are means with the source's likely ranges; scenarios are conditional, and range width is not a probability assigned to the scenario itself.

IPCC2014 Synthesis Report, Table2.1 and notes.

compares annual CO2 emissions (billions of tonnes/year) with resulting atmospheric CO2 concentrations (ppm). Low-emission paths bend down and can become net negative;

concentrations need not follow emissions one-for-one because they reflect accumulated additions and removals. The caption says only central illustrative curves are shown, not the full uncertainty distribution.

This graphic therefore illustrates scenario mechanisms rather than exact forecasts.

4.3 Climate policy: source claims and verified chronology

names the UNFCCC, Kyoto arrangements and annual Conferences of the Parties (COPs). It refers to Annex I/II groupings and writes “DCM, JI”;

the recognized Kyoto project mechanism is CDM, Clean Development Mechanism, alongside Joint Implementation. Annex groupings/targets are not all interchangeable: the official Kyoto overview distinguishes the Convention's annex structure and Kyoto Annex B commitments.

Only that overview is needed here, not an invented treaty-law curriculum. Correction: the slide's “1996 Kyoto Protocol” is wrong; Kyoto was adopted 11 December 1997 and entered into force 16 February 2005. UNFCCC Kyoto overview, opening/market mechanisms.

Consider Paris COP21 in 2015 as pursuing warming well below 2°C and efforts toward 1.5°C above preindustrial levels, with peaking as soon as possible and rapid subsequent reductions. Its discussion of INDCs concerns the pledges at that historical conference, not a checked present-day assessment of every NDC.

The slide calls 4 November 2016 a “ratification” date; it is the Agreement's entry into force date.

Adoption was 12 December 2015; individual parties ratify on their own dates. UNFCCC Paris overview.

The slide's subsequent conference list runs through COP30/Brazil 2025, naming Marrakesh 2016, Bonn 2017, Katowice 2018, Madrid 2019, the 2020 pandemic gap, Glasgow 2021, Cairo 2022, Dubai 2023 and Baku 2024. Location correction: COP27 was in Sharm el-Sheikh, Egypt, not Cairo; the official proceedings state its 2022 venue. UNFCCC COP27 proceedings listing.

These are the source's historical orientation, not a live future meeting schedule.

4.4 Mitigation and adaptation solve different parts of the problem

Mitigation reduces climate-change drivers, such as net greenhouse-gas emissions; adaptation changes exposure/vulnerability and responses to climatic effects that still occur. combines a historical/projected warming panel with five escalating risk categories—unique/threatened systems, extreme weather, distribution of impacts, global aggregate impacts and large-scale singular events.

Coloured transitions represent increasing risk, not cash damages or a uniform threshold for all outcomes. It overlays Paris NDC/aspiration labels on an AR5-era figure and concludes that even successful mitigation leaves impacts requiring adaptation.

Read the overlay as a teaching illustration, not a current quantified forecast of the Paris pledges.

The core reading path is assumptions→emissions→forcing→conditional climate→risks, then the complementary responses. Matching scenario type, baseline, date and uncertainty is prerequisite to using the numbers; conference chronology is supporting context with the source errors explicitly repaired here.

Solution: AR5changes are relative to 1986–2005. For scenario RCP2.6, the difference of displayed means is\(1.0-1.0=0.000000^\circ\mathrm{C}\);

late between-scenario mean gap is\(3.7-1.0=2.700000^\circ\mathrm{C}\). These mean differences are not new uncertainty intervals: preserve each likely range and model/natural-variability/horizon qualifications.

They are conditional projections, not assigned scenario probabilities or automatically values above preindustrial conditions. SSPs describe socioeconomic narratives;

RCPs describe forcing pathways. SSP2 alone does not uniquely select a forcing path, and SSP2-4.5 combines socioeconomic family 2 with approximate 2100 forcing 4.5 W/m².

The source’s RCP6.0/8.5 “close to SSP2” statements conflict and are not used as unique mappings. Older ppm CO2e shorthand is forcing-equivalent concentration, not CO2 alone or GWP-weighted tonnes.

The first action reduces climate drivers (mitigation); the second manages remaining exposure/vulnerability (adaptation).

Even successful mitigation leaves some impacts; the source’s coloured risk categories are neither cash damages nor one uniform threshold.

Kyoto was adopted 11 December 1997 and entered into force 16 February 2005; the mechanism is CDM with JI, not DCM.

Paris adoption 12 December 2015 and entry 4 November 2016 are distinct from each party’s ratification date. COP27 was Sharm el-Sheikh, not Cairo.

These bounded historical corrections and source COP30/Brazil 2025 pointer are not a checked live timetable or present NDC-effect assessment.

Reveal explanation

Subtract like-baseline means; retain conditional scenarios, ranges and horizons.

Reveal explanation

Distinguish actions on climate drivers from actions on exposure/vulnerability.

What does SSP 2 by itself specify?

Reveal explanation

Keep socioeconomic narrative, forcing, concentration and probability concepts separate.
