03

Motivation, intuition, and definitions

Compare same-sample simple/multiple regression output, calculate and interpret ordinary/adjusted \(R^2\), and produce a short empirical report distinguishing conditional association, significance, fit, diagnostics and missing provenance. The question is what the available information lets us calculate or conclude, and which extra conditions the conclusion needs.

Use same dependent variable and observations for the prepared fit comparison. \(SSE\) denotes residual sum of squares here; \(SST=\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2\); \(k\) counts nonconstant regressors. With intercept \(R^2=1-SSE/SST\); adjusted \(R^2=1-(SSE/(n-k -1))/(SST/(n -1))\). \(R^2\) describes sample variation around the mean accounted for by the fit, not the causal share or out-of-sample guarantee. CSV 1000 rows has no units, sampling process or generating equation; \(u\)-column name alone gives no structural-error definition.

04

Explanation steps

  1. Define research question and choose transparent specification. Simple price model summarizes its bivariate association. Multiple model price slope holds income/gasoline fixed. Read an intercept cautiously at all regressors \(0\) if outside relevant support.
  2. Report \(\text{slope}\cdot\text{one-unit change}\) with units, held-fixed variables and sign. Control-induced slope changes are compatible with omitted-variable effects, not proof the richer model removes all omitted factors. A \(t\) statistic compares estimate with a stated null relative to its conventional \(\mathrm{se}\), conditional on maintained inference assumptions; it is not causal identification/economic importance.
  3. Intercept OLS orthogonality yields \(\text{total}=\text{fitted variation}+SSE\). Hence \(R^2=1-SSE/SST\). Adding regressors to the same sample retains the old fit by setting new coefficients \(0\); minimized \(SSE\) cannot increase, \(R^2\) cannot decrease. Adjusted \(R^2\) penalizes \(\mathrm{df}\); it can fall or be negative. Changes of sample/\(y\) invalidate this simple nesting argument.
  4. Calculate adjusted fit using \(n -1\) versus \(n-k -1\). Never confuse explained sum of squares with \(SSE\) or count the intercept as a nonconstant regressor.
  5. Structure the report: research question, known variables/units and sample/provenance, justified equation, readable coefficient \(+\mathrm{se}\)+fit table, held-fixed interpretations and uncertainty, fit and diagnostic decisions, remaining assumptions and policy limits. Proposed income/private-transport or gasoline/substitution mechanisms are hypotheses, not identified results. Missing \(u\) definition prevents treating it as an ordinary structural control.
  6. This lesson compares already-prepared output/calculations; hands-on R/other-software command competence and raw-data reruns are not assessed by this plan. No optimal fare claim follows without objective/cost/capacity/causal response.

Interpret conditional associations carefully

In model (3), a one-file-unit increase in price is associated with approximately 2.704 fewer travel units, holding income and gasoline fixed. One additional income unit is associated with 0.150 fewer travel units, holding the other included variables fixed. One additional gasoline unit is associated with 0.811 more travel units. The negative price sign matches the starting hypothesis. A positive gasoline sign is compatible with more expensive driving making public transport relatively attractive. A negative income sign is compatible with substitution toward private transport as income rises, although this is a proposed mechanism, not a mechanism identified by these estimates. The dataset's unknown units prevent interpretation as dollars, percentages, or a particular number of passengers. Seminar 1, PDF p. 2, interpretation prompt; Computed from travel.csv

The price slope becomes much less negative after income is included. The specifications answer different questions: model (1) summarizes unconditional price–travel association, whereas models (2) and (3) compare observations at the same included control values. The change is compatible with omitted-variable effects in the simple model; it does not prove that model (3) has zero conditional mean or that all relevant controls have been included. The model (3) slope \(t\) statistics for zero null values are about \(-206.11\), \(-119.99\), and \(22.90\) for price, income, and gasoline. Each is far from zero relative to its conventional standard error, so all three would be significant at ordinary levels under the maintained inference assumptions. Goodness of fit: R-squared and adjusted R-squared

Problem 2 asks for both measures. Worked explanation: with an intercept, define total sum of squares \(\mathrm{SST}=\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2\) and residual sum of squares \(\mathrm{SSE}=\sum_i\hat u_i^2\). The latter notation explicitly means residual, rather than explained, sum of squares here. OLS orthogonality gives the decomposition into fitted variation around the mean and residual variation. Therefore

\[R^2=1-\frac{\mathrm{SSE}}{\mathrm{SST}}.\]

For model (3), \(\mathrm{SSE}=94{,}171.050600\) and \(\mathrm{SST}=11{,}096{,}411.524222\), so \(R^2=0.991513\). The fitted model accounts for approximately 99.1513% of the sample variation in travel around its mean. This is a description of sample fit; it is not a statement that the included variables causally explain that share, nor a guarantee of performance on new data. Seminar 1, PDF p. 2, Problem 2; Computed from travel.csv

Adding regressors to the same OLS sample cannot increase the minimized \(SSE\): the original model remains available by setting each new coefficient to zero. Thus \(R^2\) cannot decrease merely because more regressors were allowed. Adjusted \(R^2\) accounts for residual degrees of freedom:

\[\bar R^2 =1-\frac{\mathrm{SSE}/(n-k-1)}{\mathrm{SST}/(n-1)} =1-(1-R^2)\frac{n-1}{n-k-1},\]

where \(k\) counts nonconstant regressors. It can fall if the reduction in \(SSE\) is too small to compensate for the additional parameters, and can be negative for a poorly fitting model. Comparisons should use the same dependent variable and observations. Here both fit measures rise across the three specifications, but that alone cannot determine whether a coefficient has a causal interpretation. For the ten-city regression, \(R^2=0.340477\) and adjusted \(R^2=0.258037\); these are separate results from a separate dataset. Seminar 1, PDF p. 2, Problem 2

Misconceptions and correction

Sign: treating 99%\(R^2\) as 99% causal explanation, assuming adjusted fit cannot fall, reading unknown units as percent/dollars, or calling all controls as proof of exogeneity. Correction: sample fit is \(SSE\) relative to \(SST\); the adjustment compares variation adjusted for degrees of freedom; held-fixed comparison still needs mean assumptions and provenance. Hint1: label \(SSE\) as residual and write both denominators. Hint2: separate sample fit, conditional association and causal claim in three sentences.

Score the original response before help. One ambiguous error only suggests a tentative misconception; establish it only from explicit reasoning or repeated diagnostic signs. Explain the relevant prepared step and its consequence; never label the learner from confidence or a single slip.
