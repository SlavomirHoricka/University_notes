Explanation steps

Source passage

Origin: reused complete checked passage MA(1): dependence with a finite reach; fingerprint 6f5ada6d78aec79c0f8d3419b4cd89e9fa50e130af71d6ca9b0958a4c57bb974. Heading depth alone is adapted.

MA(1): dependence with a finite reach

Let

\[x_t=\varepsilon_t+\alpha\varepsilon_{t-1},\]

where innovations \(\varepsilon_t\) are i.i.d., have mean zero and finite positive variance \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\). This is a data-generating process, not necessarily a regression equation. The same fixed transformation of identically distributed innovation blocks at every date gives strict stationarity. Independence eliminates cross terms, so

\[E[x_t]=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(x_t)=(1+\alpha^2)\sigma_\varepsilon^2.\]

Adjacent values share \(\varepsilon_t\):

\[\operatorname{Cov}(x_t,x_{t+1})=\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_t+\alpha\varepsilon_{t-1},\varepsilon_{t+1}+\alpha\varepsilon_t)=\alpha\sigma_\varepsilon^2,\]

\[\operatorname{Corr}(x_t,x_{t+1})=\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha^2}.\]

At lag two or more, the innovation sets are disjoint, hence the values are independent under the stated i.i.d. assumption. Dependence ends after one lag; it need not be zero at lag one for the process to be weakly dependent. No \(|\alpha|<1\) restriction is needed for this stationarity argument; a different invertibility question is not part of this lecture. 2026_Lecture3_print.pdf, physical PDF p. 11, 14 Wooldridge, Introductory Econometrics, 5th ed., printed p. 383 (PDF p. 411)

05

Worked example

O015-W1, item_version 1, worked_example. Prompt: Let \(x_t=\varepsilon_t+\alpha\varepsilon_{t-1}\) for an i.i.d. innovation sequence defined at all required integer dates, \(E[\varepsilon_t]=0\), \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t)=4>0\), and \(\alpha=0.5\). Explain the MA(1) DGP and derive its mean, variance, lag-one covariance/correlation, and lag-2 dependence by expanding the innovation terms. Assess strict/covariance stationarity and weak dependence. Is \(|\alpha|<1\) required here? Does zero lag-2 covariance alone generally prove independence, and why can independence be concluded for these particular disjoint blocks?

Complete solution and essential rubric: This is a data-generating moving average, not automatically a regression interpretation. \(E[x_t]=0\); independence removes cross terms, so \(\gamma(0)=(1+\alpha^2)\sigma_\varepsilon^2=5.00000000\). Adjacent expressions share only \(\varepsilon_t\), with coefficient one in \(x_t\) and \(\alpha\) in \(x_{t+1}\), so \(\gamma(1)=\alpha\sigma_\varepsilon^2=2.00000000\) and \(\rho(1)=\alpha/(1+\alpha^2)=0.40000000\). At lag 2 the blocks \(\{\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-1}\}\) and \(\{\varepsilon_{t+2},\varepsilon_{t+2-1}\}\) are disjoint; the i.i.d. assumption implies independent blocks and therefore independent functions, with covariance/correlation zero. The same transformation of identically distributed blocks at every shift gives strict stationarity and finite moments give covariance stationarity. Dependence ends after one lag, so it satisfies the finite-reach weak-dependence example even if adjacent values correlate. No \(|\alpha|<1\) condition is needed for finite MA stationarity; invertibility is a separate unassessed question. Zero covariance in general does not imply independence; here the independent innovation blocks justify it.
