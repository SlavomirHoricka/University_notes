Introduction

In this lesson, derive MA(1) moments and finite-reach dependence.

2.1 What you’ll learn

  • Derive MA(1) moments and dependence from independent innovation blocks, preserving the distinction between stationarity and invertibility.

2.2 Before you begin

  • Distinguish stationarity from weak dependence and limit-theorem roles
Check the foundations

Define covariance stationarity and explain why zero covariance alone need not imply independent random variables.

Reveal foundation explanation

Constant finite mean/variance and lag-only covariance are required; covariance tests only linear co-movement and does not establish general independence.

03

Key definitions and concepts

  • MA(1): A process combining the current innovation with a weighted previous innovation.
  • Innovation: The specified independent shock entering the process.
  • Autocorrelation: Lag covariance divided by the common variance in the stationary case.
04

MA(1): dependence with a finite reach

Let

\[x_t=\varepsilon_t+\alpha\varepsilon_{t-1},\]

where innovations \(\varepsilon_t\) are i.i.d., have mean zero and finite positive variance \(\sigma_\varepsilon^2\). This is a data-generating process, not necessarily a regression equation. The same fixed transformation of identically distributed innovation blocks at every date gives strict stationarity. Independence eliminates cross terms, so

\[E[x_t]=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(x_t)=(1+\alpha^2)\sigma_\varepsilon^2.\]

Adjacent values share \(\varepsilon_t\):

\[\operatorname{Cov}(x_t,x_{t+1})=\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_t+\alpha\varepsilon_{t-1},\varepsilon_{t+1}+\alpha\varepsilon_t)=\alpha\sigma_\varepsilon^2,\]
\[\operatorname{Corr}(x_t,x_{t+1})=\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha^2}.\]

At lag two or more, the innovation sets are disjoint, hence the values are independent under the stated i.i.d. assumption. Dependence ends after one lag; it need not be zero at lag one for the process to be weakly dependent. No \(|\alpha|<1\) restriction is needed for this stationarity argument; a different invertibility question is not part of this lecture.

05

Worked example

Prompt: Let \(x_t=\varepsilon_t+\alpha\varepsilon_{t-1}\) for an i.i.d. innovation sequence defined at all required integer dates, \(E[\varepsilon_t]=0\), \(\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t)=4>0\), and \(\alpha=0.5\). Explain the MA(1) DGP and derive its mean, variance, lag-one covariance/correlation, and lag-2 dependence by expanding the innovation terms.

Assess strict/covariance stationarity and weak dependence. Is \(|\alpha|<1\) required here?

Does zero lag-2 covariance alone generally prove independence, and why can independence be concluded for these particular disjoint blocks?

Solution: This is a data-generating moving average, not automatically a regression interpretation. \(E[x_t]=0\); independence removes cross terms, so \(\gamma(0)=(1+\alpha^2)\sigma_\varepsilon^2=5.00000000\).

Adjacent expressions share only \(\varepsilon_t\), with coefficient one in \(x_t\) and \(\alpha\) in \(x_{t+1}\), so \(\gamma(1)=\alpha\sigma_\varepsilon^2=2.00000000\) and \(\rho(1)=\alpha/(1+\alpha^2)=0.40000000\). At lag 2 the blocks \(\{\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-1}\}\) and \(\{\varepsilon_{t+2},\varepsilon_{t+2-1}\}\) are disjoint;

the i.i.d. assumption implies independent blocks and therefore independent functions, with covariance/correlation zero. The same transformation of identically distributed blocks at every shift gives strict stationarity and finite moments give covariance stationarity.

Dependence ends after one lag, so it satisfies the finite-reach weak-dependence example even if adjacent values correlate. No \(|\alpha|<1\) condition is needed for finite MA stationarity;

invertibility is a separate unassessed question. Zero covariance in general does not imply independence;

here the independent innovation blocks justify it.

Check your reasoning: an offline quiz

Choose one answer per question. Check individual answers or check the whole quiz. Each option includes feedback on the reasoning, and everything works offline.

7.1 Question 1

\(x_t=\varepsilon_t+0.5\varepsilon_{t-1}\), iid innovation variance 4. What is variance?

Reveal explanation

Independent components give \(4(1+0.5^2)=5\).

7.2 Question 2

What are lag 1 covariance and correlation?

Reveal explanation

The common innovation contributes \(0.5\times 4=2\); divide by 5 for correlation.

7.3 Question 3

Why are observations two dates apart independent in this particular construction?

Reveal explanation

The iid innovation blocks supply the stronger independence argument; covariance alone would not.
