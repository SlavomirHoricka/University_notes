Introduction

In this lesson, separate consistency from usual asymptotic inference.

  • Derive the usual large-sample variance/test bridge under the added restrictions
  • Distinguish it from exact finite-sample inference and unsupported later corrections.

Which TS1a–3a result follows from the normalized score LLN? What covariance terms does a usual variance formula omit? Does adding a deterministic trend make a random walk weakly dependent?

Reveal foundation explanation

TS1a–3a with suitable product LLNs and nonsingular \(Q\) gives consistency. Serial error covariance terms are omitted by the usual no-serial-correlation formula, distinct from mere weak dependence. A deterministic trend does not remove accumulated random-walk innovations. Require all clauses;

03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Asymptotic inference: A large-sample sampling approximation under the stated limit-theorem conditions.
  • Score CLT: A limiting distribution for the scaled regressor-error score sum.
  • Usual variance approximation: The classical large-sample variance under the extra homoskedastic/no-relevant-serial-error restrictions.
04

Additional assumptions for usual inference

The lecture adds

\[\text{TS4a: }\operatorname{Var}(u_t\mid x_t)=\sigma^2,\qquad \text{TS5a: }E[u_tu_s\mid x_t,x_s]=0\quad(t\ne s).\]

TS4a is contemporaneous homoskedasticity; TS5a restricts serial error correlation conditional on the paired regressor sets. Neither follows simply because \(x_t\) is stationary. Weak dependence permits some serial dependence in the data, while TS5a rules out the error dependence relevant to ordinary OLS standard errors. They are different conditions.

Under TS1a–TS5a and the applicable CLT/moments, the lecture states asymptotic normality and validity of ordinary OLS standard errors and \(t\), \(F\), LM statistics. To see the bridge, multiply the consistency identity by \(\sqrt n\): the scaled score average \((1/\sqrt n)\sum z_tu_t\) has a limiting normal distribution;

the design factor approaches \(Q^{-1}\). Under the stated homoskedastic/no-serial-correlation restrictions, its resulting covariance is \(\sigma^2Q^{-1}\).

Thus the familiar estimated variance \(\widehat\sigma^2(Z'Z)^{-1}\) can support large-sample inference. This is an explanatory consequence of the stated theorem under its regularity, not an exact distribution in every finite sample.

For a coefficient null, \((\widehat\beta_j-\beta_{j,0})/\operatorname{se}(\widehat\beta_j)\) has the usual large-sample normal approximation. A claim of an exact finite-sample Student distribution still needs stronger assumptions.

Normality of the error itself is not required for this asymptotic route. If TS4a or TS5a fails, consistency may survive under TS1a–TS3a, but the usual standard-error/inference guarantee does not.

Corrections for heteroskedasticity and serial correlation are next week's advertised Chapter12 topic, outside this ingestion.

05

Trend and persistence qualifications

For a truly trend-stationary specification, suitable deterministic trends can be included and the remaining stochastic components must satisfy the relevant dependence/exogeneity/inference conditions. A time trend does not automatically cure a stochastic unit root.

For highly persistent/unit-root data, the usual weak-dependence asymptotic argument may fail; the random-walk note explains why differences can help.

The source also correctly distinguishes the theoretical possibility of exact finite-sample CLM inference under its full strong assumptions from the vulnerability of that inference when assumptions fail.

says OLS is asymptotically efficient under its assumptions, but gives no efficiency bound or estimator class. That statement should not be generalized to efficiency against all estimators in every dynamic model;

the checked usable results here are the explicit consistency and usual-inference theorems. requests revision of the OLS variance derivation; the local asymptotic variance bridge above connects to the full checked Week2 simple-slope variance derivation and multiple-regression precision explanation.

Worked example

Prompt: A time-series regression has a valid TS1a–3a consistency argument. For one coefficient suppose in a hypothetical large-sample calculation the orthogonal design component has \(Q\) diagonal entry \(q=4\) (so its inverse entry is \(1/q\)), error variance \(\sigma^2=9\), sample size \(n=100\), estimate \(\widehat\beta_j=0.5\) and null \(\beta_{j,0}=0\).

Under the additional stated lecture inference assumptions and applicable score CLT/moments, derive the scaled-normal covariance and ordinary variance approximation, then compute an approximate standard error and standardized statistic. State TS4a and TS5a precisely, contrast weak dependence of data with no relevant serial error covariance, and explain what survives or fails if either inference condition fails.

Distinguish asymptotic from exact finite-sample inference, normality and broad efficiency claims; explain why including a trend cannot cure an arbitrary unit root.

Solution: TS4a requires \(\operatorname{Var}(u_t\mid x_t)=\sigma^2\); TS5a requires \(E[u_tu_s\mid x_t,x_s]=0\) for distinct dates.

They are additional error restrictions, not consequences of stationary or weakly dependent regressors. Multiply the consistency identity by \(\sqrt n\);

the score CLT plus the convergent inverse design gives limiting covariance \(\sigma^2Q^{-1}\) under these restrictions. Thus \(\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\beta_j)\approx\sigma^2/(nq)\) in this supplied scalar/orthogonal component illustration; \(\operatorname{se}\approx\sqrt{9/(100(4))}=0.15000000\) and \((\widehat\beta_j-\beta_{j,0})/\operatorname{se}=3.33333333\).

These are declared large-sample approximations, not an exact finite-sample Student law or actual data estimate. Full estimated variance uses \(\widehat\sigma^2(Z\prime Z)^{-1}\) and its applicable assumptions.

TS1a–3a consistency may survive heteroskedasticity/serial error correlation, while the usual variance/test guarantee need not. Weak dependence permits some serial dependence in the joint data;

TS5a addresses the error products relevant to ordinary standard errors. An error-normality assumption is unnecessary for this asymptotic route;

exact finite-sample \(t\) and \(F\) needs stronger classical conditions. The lecture supplies no estimator class/bound for a universal efficiency claim.

A correct deterministic trend can remove that deterministic component, but not accumulated stochastic unit-root shocks; formal robust corrections and unit-root tests remain outside this lesson.

Check your reasoning: an offline quiz

Choose one answer per question. Check individual answers or check the whole quiz. Each option includes feedback on the reasoning, and everything works offline.

9.1 Question 1

sigma² 9, n 100 and orthogonal design component q 4. What is approximate coefficient SE?

Reveal explanation

The approximate standard error is \(\sqrt{\frac{9}{100\times4}}=0.15\), with the square root applied to the variance.

9.2 Question 2

Estimate 0.5, null 0 and approximate SE 0.15 give what standardized statistic?

Reveal explanation

Use (0.5−0)/0.15.

9.3 Question 3

If the additional ordinary-variance conditions fail but the stated consistency assumptions remain, what can follow?

Reveal explanation

Keep TS 1a–3a consistency separate from additional score-CLT and ordinary covariance restrictions.
