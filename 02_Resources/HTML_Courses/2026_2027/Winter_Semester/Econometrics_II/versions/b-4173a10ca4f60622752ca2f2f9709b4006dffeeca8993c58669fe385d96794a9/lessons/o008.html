03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Jarque–Bera statistic: A skewness/kurtosis-based normality diagnostic.
  • Auxiliary LM statistic: The stated sample size times the auxiliary regression R².
  • Diagnostic scope: The particular alternative a diagnostic addresses, rather than every model assumption.

3.1 Notation and distinctions

The finished note explicitly selects these diagnostic methods to implement an open-ended seminar task; the handout did not name them.

Jarque–Bera uses skewness \(S\) and kurtosis \(K\) with normal benchmarks \(0\) and \(3\). Homoskedasticity is constant conditional disturbance variance;

the studentized BP/Koenker auxiliary regression explains squared residuals by an intercept and selected regressors. These are large-sample tests with their own conditions, not exact proofs of population assumptions.

04

Assumption checks: what diagnostics can establish

Problem 3 requests plots and formal tests of residual normality and homoskedasticity. The following Selected diagnostic methods implement that open-ended instruction for model (3).

They are not named in the handout. Tests are interpreted at a stated 5% significance level and use large-sample reference distributions;

failing to reject is evidence of compatibility, not proof of an assumption. Residuals estimate disturbances and are constrained by OLS fitting, so tests based on them require their own statistical justification.

Computed diagnostic plots from all CSV observations for the price–income–gasoline model. The histogram is roughly symmetric;

the normal Q–Q plot compares sorted standardized residuals with standard-normal quantiles (red diagonal). Residuals against fitted values show no conspicuous funnel.

Squared residuals show the remaining spread more directly. These are calculated visual evidence, not figures in the supplied PDF. Data: travel.csv;

4.1 Normality: a plot and a moment test

A Q–Q plot matches ordered standardized residuals to normal quantiles: an approximately straight diagonal is compatible with a normal shape, while curvature and tail departures indicate shape differences. The displayed residual histogram is broadly bell-shaped, and the Q–Q points lie near the diagonal, with some tail departures. Neither visual establishes exact conditional normality.

For a formal check, the Jarque–Bera statistic compares sample skewness \(S\) and kurtosis \(K\) with their normal-distribution values of zero and three. Using centered residual moments \(m_r=n^{-1}\sum_i\hat u_i^r\), set \(S=m_3/m_2^{3/2}\) and \(K=m_4/m_2^2\). Residual means are zero apart from numerical rounding because the model has an intercept. The statistic is

\[\mathrm{JB}=n\left(\frac{S^2}{6}+\frac{(K-3)^2}{24}\right).\]

Under the usual large-sample conditions and a normal null, its reference distribution is \(\chi^2_2\). Here \(S=0.031493\), \(K=3.051706\), \(\mathrm{JB}=0.276697\), and the asymptotic \(p\) value is \(0.870795\).

We do not reject normality at 5%. This is a selected moment-based check, not proof of all aspects of conditional normality.

These definitions and results are the agent's worked method for the source's requested normality test. ; Calculated from travel.csv

4.2 Homoskedasticity: a spread plot and an auxiliary regression

Homoskedasticity means constant conditional disturbance variance. A widening or narrowing residual band against fitted values would raise concerns, while roughly similar spread is compatible with constant variance. The plots show no obvious systematic funnel, but a single projection can miss variance patterns associated with individual regressors.

For a formal check, use an auxiliary regression of squared residuals on an intercept and the three included regressors. The studentized Breusch–Pagan / Koenker form tests whether those regressors explain residual variance.

If its \(R^2\) is \(R^2_{\mathrm{aux}}\), its statistic is \(\mathrm{LM}=nR^2_{\mathrm{aux}}\), with a large-sample \(\chi^2_3\) reference under the homoskedastic null and the usual independent-sampling and moment conditions. The three degrees of freedom count the nonconstant auxiliary regressors.

Here \(R^2_{\mathrm{aux}}=0.004737882\), \(\mathrm{LM}=4.737882\), and \(p\approx0.192028\). We do not reject homoskedasticity at 5% against this particular alternative. A different auxiliary specification could detect different variance patterns. This is the Selected solution method for the handout's homoskedasticity task, rather than a theorem or test named by the handout. ; Calculated from travel.csv

4.3 Assumptions that remain substantive

The sample design matrix can be checked for full rank; the three CSV specifications have full rank and hence unique OLS coefficients.

Functional form remains a modeling choice. The file alone does not document random sampling or representativeness, and residual orthogonality cannot verify population exogeneity.

Adding controls may improve a plausible specification, but omitted causes, simultaneity, or measurement error could still invalidate the desired interpretation. Passing residual diagnostics therefore does not prove OLS unbiasedness, consistency, or causality.

Those properties follow from the conditions developed in (unbiasedness; consistency; efficiency and inference), not from a high \(R^2\). ;

Connect the reasoning

  1. Inspect residual histogram/normal Q–Q plot: diagonal compatibility supports a normal-shaped description; curvature/tail departures raise concerns. The recorded plot is broadly symmetric/near diagonal with some tails. Residuals are estimated disturbances constrained by OLS, not direct iid draws of true \(u\).
  2. Centered residual moments \(m_r=n^{-1}\sum_i\hat u_i^r\) yield \(S=m_3/m_2^{3/2}\), \(K=m_4/m_2^2\). \(JB=n[S^2/6+(K-3)^2/24]\), with \(\chi^2_2\) large-sample reference under normal-null/regularity. \(S=0, K=3\) yields \(0\), but matching two moments cannot prove all conditional-distribution properties.
  3. Residuals versus fitted values and squared residuals versus fitted show spread: funnel suggests variance concern. The recorded image has no conspicuous funnel, but a single projection can miss regressor-specific patterns. Regress squared residuals on intercept plus \(q\) nonconstant chosen regressors: \(LM=nR_{\mathrm{aux}}^2\), asymptotic \(\chi^2_q\) under homoskedastic null/independent sampling/moment conditions. \(q\) excludes the intercept; auxiliary specification determines the alternatives probed.
  4. Fix \(\alpha=0.05\) before interpreting supplied \(p\). If \(p<0.05\) reject null; otherwise do not reject. Nonrejection means this test found insufficient contrary evidence; it is not acceptance/proof. No \(p\) value is computed from a invented reference table; prepared \(p\) values are part of the prompts.
  5. Neither diagram/test establishes random sampling, representativeness, population exogeneity, causality or structural definition of the CSV \(u\) column. Sample full rank can establish unique coefficients, but functional form and mean assumptions remain substantive. A good fit and passing diagnostics cannot turn a regression into a causal model.

Check your understanding

Avoid these mistakes: saying p 0.87 is 87% probability normality is true, counting intercept in LMdf, or using normality test to prove exogeneity. Correction: p is not the posterior probability of the null; compare it with alpha, count tested nonconstant auxiliary regressors, and separate mean assumptions from shape/spread.
