04

Course purpose and preparation

This is the second undergraduate econometrics course. Its purpose is to enable independent empirical work: translate a research question into a regression equation, select a data structure and method, estimate it in statistical software, assess the assumptions, and interpret the results.

The lecture emphasizes understanding the data-generating process because economic data do not automatically meet the assumptions of an exact statistical model. The SIS export lists 6 ECTS, Econometrics I (JEB109) as a prerequisite, and JEB125 as a course for which this course is a prerequisite. ;

The lecturer is Barbara Pertold-Gebicka; the seminar leaders are Veronika Plachá, Oliver Škultéty, and Kai Wang.

The uploaded schedule gives Wednesday lectures at 12:30–13:50 and seminars on Wednesday at 15:30–16:50 or 18:30–19:50, or Thursday at 14:00–15:20. The lecture lists room 016 for seminars.

Prepare by reading the assigned material and revising prerequisite methods before the lecture or seminar. Week 1's lecture is September 30, and the seminar handout is dated September 30 / October 1, 2026. ; ;

The main textbook is Jeffrey M. Wooldridge's Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach, fourth edition or higher according to the lecture.

The lecture recommends Chris Brooks, Angrist and Pischke, and Cameron and Trivedi as complementary readings; the SIS export lists Brooks and Cameron and Trivedi.

These are reading references, not sources ingested in this pass. R is the official seminar software.

The lecture permits other software in home assignments, while the syllabus and SIS export request appended R code; students should clarify expectations if using another package. ; ;

05

Assessment and submitted work

Four assignments are worth up to 10 points each (40 total), the midterm up to 20, and the final up to 40. Passing requires more than 20 final-exam points;

the lecture expresses this as at least 21 points and more than 50% of the final. A good overall total cannot replace this separate final-exam requirement.

The midterm is one-shot; Consider November 11, 2026, at 12:30.

The final takes place in the exam period, and the lecture permits three trials. ;

Assignments combine a theoretical problem and an empirical problem treated as a small project with a short report. Lecture 1 says “Work in groups of 2 students,” whereas the syllabus and SIS export encourage groups of two;

the source wording differs in whether pairing is expressed as a requirement or encouragement. The syllabus says each such group submits one solution naming both participants, appends R code, and meets the stated deadline because late work receives no credit.

The lecture promises assignment feedback within 10 working days and exam scores within 7 working days. ;

The midterm uses multiple-choice and YES/NO questions according to Lecture 1. Its stated scope is the first six lectures, testing theoretical and practical knowledge.

The final covers the whole course and includes true/false, open theoretical, and open practical questions. The SIS export explicitly describes both exams as compulsory and in person.

These statements record the supplied exam formats and scope; they do not ingest the later lecture content. ; ;
