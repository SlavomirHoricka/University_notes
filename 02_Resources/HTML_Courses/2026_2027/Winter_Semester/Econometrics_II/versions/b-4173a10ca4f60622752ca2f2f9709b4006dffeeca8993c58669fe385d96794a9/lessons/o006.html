Reveal foundation explanation

Slope \(S_{xy}/S_{xx} = -1.5\); \(\text{intercept}=20-(-1.5)\cdot 5=27.5\); residual is actual \(y\) minus fitted \(y\).

Hypothetical simple-regression summary: \(n=12, k=1\) nonconstant regressor with intercept, \(SSE=80, S_{xx}=20\). Give residual \(\mathrm{df}\), estimated noise variance, estimated slope variance and standard error. Name the full assumptions needed for the stated exact classical null \(t\) reference and its \(\mathrm{df}\).

Reveal foundation explanation

Residual \(\mathrm{df}=n-k-1=10\); estimated noise variance \(\hat\sigma^2=80/10=8\); estimated slope variance \(\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\beta_1)=8/20=0.4\); \(\mathrm{se}=\sqrt{0.4}\), approximately \(0.632456\). Exact null \(t(10)\) requires all six classical assumptions: linearity in parameters, random sampling, no perfect collinearity, zero conditional mean, homoskedasticity and conditional normality. Merely calling residuals orthogonal does not establish the population assumptions.

03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Conditional prediction: A fitted outcome associated with specified regressor values.
  • Extrapolation: Prediction outside the observed regressor range.
  • Nonrejection: Insufficient evidence against the specified null at the chosen test level.

3.1 Notation and distinctions

A fare-demand linear specification imposes a constant fitted change per fare unit. Expected negative fare sign is a starting hypothesis, not a source-mandated law.

Potential omitted factors include population/density, service quality, car availability, income/driving cost; if they affect demand and co-vary with fare, zero conditional mean can fail.

The ten-city table explicitly labels \(y\) as passengers per hour(thousand), \(x\) dollars: preserve this literal scale even when surprising. The CSV uses undocumented file units and is a separate dataset.

Optimal fare requires a defined objective, costs/capacity and credible causal response, absent here.

04

From economic intuition to a model

Problem 1(i) asks about the sign and shape of demand and other influences. A plausible starting hypothesis is that a higher fare reduces public-transport use, holding relevant alternatives and service conditions fixed.

A straight line imposes a constant change in demand per unit price; a curved or logarithmic relation would impose a different shape.

The handout leaves the shape to discussion, so the linear model used below is a transparent choice for revising OLS, not a source-mandated demand law.

An initial specification is

\[\text{travel}_i=\beta_0+\beta_1\text{price}_i+u_i.\]

The slope is the change in the model's systematic travel demand associated with a one-unit increase in fare. The intercept is its value at zero fare.

Other potential influences include city population and density, service frequency and quality, car availability, income, and the cost of driving. These are economic hypotheses offered to answer the prompt, not variables all observed in the supplied data.

If such factors both affect demand and vary systematically with fare, putting them in \(u_i\) can violate zero conditional mean. A negative estimated price coefficient alone therefore does not identify a causal demand curve. ;

The manager's ultimate objective is an optimal fare, but the exercise only estimates demand and evaluates the regression. Choosing an optimal fare would additionally require a defined objective, costs or capacity constraints, and a credible estimate of how fare changes affect demand.

Those inputs are not supplied; no optimum is claimed here.

This limitation follows from the distinction between the manager's motivating question and the tasks actually specified in Problem 1.

05

Ten-city example: keep the source's measurement scale

The printed table labels the dependent variable “Passengers per hour (thousand)” and the fare “Fair price (dollars).” The values below preserve the printed numbers literally. The large passenger scale is not silently corrected.

Thus a unit of \(y\) means one unit on the table's stated thousand-passengers-per-hour scale, and a unit of price means one dollar. The later CSV has a different numeric scale and no supplied unit dictionary, so its results must not be assigned these table units automatically.

Source crop: the original ten-city table, including its unit labels. It supplies the observations for the following calculations.

5.1 Estimate the line

For the ten cities, the calculated sample means and centered sums are

\[\bar x=0.93,\qquad\bar y=1894,\qquad S_{xx}=\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2=1.031,\]
\[S_{xy}=\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)=-3579.7.\]

Applying the lecture's OLS formula gives

\[\hat\beta_1=\frac{-3579.7}{1.031}=-3472.065955, \qquad \hat\beta_0=1894-(-3472.065955)(0.93)=5123.021339.\]

Hence \(\widehat{\text{travel}}_i=5123.021339-3472.065955\text{price}_i\). A fare difference of $0.10 is associated with a fitted demand difference of \(-347.206596\) units on the printed passenger scale.

This is a cross-city association in the chosen linear specification. The estimated intercept is outside the observed fare range, $0.50–$1.50, and should not be interpreted as observed demand under a zero-fare policy.

Calculations answer Problem 1(iii) using the source table and Lecture 1's centered estimator. ;

5.3 Disturbance variance, slope variance, and the t statistic

The true disturbances \(u_i\) and their variance \(\sigma^2\) are unknown. In a simple regression with an intercept, the conventional variance estimator uses \(n-2\) residual degrees of freedom:

\[\hat\sigma^2=\frac{\sum_i\hat u_i^2}{n-2} =\frac{24{,}075{,}579.500}{8} =3{,}009{,}447.437.\]

Two estimated coefficients consume two degrees of freedom. Under the homoskedastic model, the slope's theoretical conditional variance is \(\sigma^2/S_{xx}\); replacing the unknown \(\sigma^2\) with \(\hat\sigma^2\) estimates that variance. The corresponding calculated standard error is

\[\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\beta_1\mid X)=\frac{\hat\sigma^2}{1.031}, \qquad \operatorname{se}(\hat\beta_1)=1708.496323.\]

For \(H_0:\beta_1=0\), \(t=-3472.065955/1708.496323=-2.032235\). An exact two-sided test compares its absolute value with the appropriate \(t_8\) critical value, under the full classical assumptions.

At the conventional 5% level it does not reject zero: \(|t|\) is below the approximately 2.306 critical value. This does not prove no relationship;

ten observations and substantial residual variation leave considerable uncertainty. The formulas and computation are the worked solution to the handout's variance and \(t\)-statistic tasks, interpreted using the lecture's inference statement. ;

06

Connect the reasoning

  1. Specify \(\mathrm{travel}=\beta_0+\beta_1\mathrm{fare}+u\); interpret slope in \(y\)-units per dollar, zero-fare intercept only if relevant. Fix the dataset before calculation; never import CSV units into the table.
  2. Obtain mean \(x\), mean \(y\), \(S_{xx}\), \(S_{xy}\); divide \(S_{xy}/S_{xx}\) and solve intercept. Use fitted difference \(\text{slope}\cdot\text{fare change}\) rather than percentage language for a level-level model.
  3. For each city compute fitted and actual-minus-fitted. Check residual sum and fare-weighted residual sum \(\approx 0\), allowing rounding. These are fitting checks, not assumption evidence.
  4. \(SSE\) is sum of squared fitted residuals; divide by \(n -2\) for simple regression with intercept. Slope variance estimate \(\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\beta_1)=\hat\sigma^2/S_{xx}\); \(\mathrm{se}\) is square root; \(t=\text{slope}/\mathrm{se}\) for zero null. Under all six assumptions compare \(\lvert t\rvert\) with the appropriate two-sided critical value from the \(t_{n-2}\) distribution; report nonrejection as uncertainty, not proof of no association.
  5. Inspect impossible fitted values, very large residuals and intercept extrapolation. The ten-city negative fitted travel at observed fare \(1.50\) signals linear-specification limitations. It does not license deleting observations. Economic explanations are hypotheses requiring evidence.
  6. Interpret the result as sample association unless exogeneity/sampling conditions are supported. Regression alone cannot choose an optimal fare.
