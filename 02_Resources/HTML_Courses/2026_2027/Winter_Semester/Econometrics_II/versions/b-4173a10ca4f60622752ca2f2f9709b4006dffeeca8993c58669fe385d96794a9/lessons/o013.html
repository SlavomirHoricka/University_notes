03

Key definitions and concepts

  • Seasonal indicator: A binary regressor for a recurring calendar position.
  • Reference season: The omitted calendar position represented by the intercept.
  • Dummy-variable trap: Exact collinearity from including a full indicator set together with an intercept.
04

Seasonality is a repeating calendar pattern

Monthly or quarterly data may have regular within-year differences. The source's example is retail sales tending to jump in the fourth quarter. Such a pattern differs from a trend: a trend describes systematic movement over time, whereas seasonality repeats with calendar position. It may be represented by seasonal dummy variables or removed before substantive regression by seasonal adjustment.

Explanation of the seasonal-dummy method: for quarterly data, include an intercept and three indicators, taking quarter 1 as reference:

\[y_t=\beta_0+\beta_1x_t+\lambda_2Q_{2t} +\lambda_3Q_{3t}+\lambda_4Q_{4t}+u_t.\]

Here \(Q_{jt}=1\) if date \(t\) lies in quarter \(j\), otherwise zero. Coefficient \(\lambda_4\) is the fourth-quarter intercept difference relative to quarter 1, holding \(x_t\) fixed.

Including all four indicators alongside an intercept would make the intercept their exact sum and violate TS3. Likewise, a model with monthly indicators and an intercept uses eleven independent indicators and one reference month.

These designs assume stable additive seasonal differences; writing them does not prove that this is the appropriate adjustment for every dataset. ;

The textbook extends the same partialling-out logic to seasonality: regress the outcome and every substantive regressor on the same intercept and seasonal indicators, then regress their residuals on each other. Their slopes agree with the model that includes those seasonal indicators.

A series may contain both a trend and seasonal pattern, so the residualization can include both sets of controls together. This is a simple regression-based adjustment, not a claim that it reproduces every statistical agency's seasonal-adjustment procedure.

The supplied lecture gives no numerical seasonal regression or seasonal-adjustment algorithm. It introduces the choice between explicit calendar controls and adjustment of the series before regression; it does not authorize inferring that a supplied series has already been adjusted.

05

What the adjustments establish

Including a trend or consistently detrending variables changes the comparison from a raw association in levels to a relationship conditional on the chosen time pattern. Seasonal indicators similarly separate stable calendar differences.

These are specification tools whose usefulness depends on the relevant time pattern being represented appropriately. They do not by themselves establish causal identification, strict exogeneity, or no serial correlation.

06

Worked example

Prompt: Hypothetical quarterly additive seasonal model with \(4\) calendar positions and intercept, reference Q1, one substantive \(x\), no trend. Intercept \(10\), \(x\) slope \(2\), \(x=3\);

the target nonreference calendar position's effect is \(5\), \(\text{time index}=8\), usable \(T=24\). Specify independent dummy count/coding and interpretation versus reference, calculate target and reference predictions at the same \(x\)/time, and full residual \(\mathrm{df}\) under conventional assumptions.

Explain why all \(4\) dummies plus intercept fail, how equivalent residualisation must treat \(y\) and all substantive regressors (including a trend when specified), and the stable-seasonal/identification/agency-adjustment limits.

Steps and interpretation: Use intercept and \(3\) calendar indicators, one omitted reference Q1; each target indicator is \(1\) only for its position.

Target effect \(5\) is an intercept difference from reference at held-fixed \(x\)/time. Target \(\text{prediction}=10+2\times 3+5+0\times 8=21\); \(\text{reference}=16\).

All \(4\) seasonal indicators sum to \(1\), the constant intercept column; this creates exact collinearity;

dropping a reference restores this seasonal basis but full design still needs rank. \(\text{Coefficient count}=4\) seasonal/intercept \(+1\) substantive slope, \(\mathrm{df}=19\). For equal slopes, residualise outcome and every substantive regressor against the same intercept/seasonal controls and same rows;

if a trend is included, use that same joint control basis for all. Then regress residuals;

preserve full-model \(\mathrm{df}\)/inference assumptions. Stable additive seasonal differences are a maintained model, not established merely by dummy inclusion.

Trend is movement over time, seasonality repeats with calendar position. Regression adjustment is not every agency's procedure and does not prove preadjusted data, strict exogeneity, no serial correlation or causality.

Check your reasoning: an offline quiz

Choose one answer per question. Check individual answers or check the whole quiz. Each option includes feedback on the reasoning, and everything works offline.

8.1 Question 1

Quarterly seasonality with an intercept requires how many independent seasonal dummies?

Reveal explanation

Use one omitted reference among four calendar positions.

8.2 Question 2

Intercept 10, x slope 2, x 3, target seasonal effect 5. What are target and reference predictions?

Reveal explanation

Reference \(10+2\times 3=16\); target adds 5.

8.3 Question 3

24 rows, quarterly intercept/seasonal basis and one substantive slope. What is residual df?

Reveal explanation

There are 4 independent seasonal/intercept coefficients plus 1 slope.
