# Temporary and Permanent Policy in the DD-AA Model

## Framework Assumptions

Two key distinctions define policy scenarios in the [[DD_AA_Model]]:

1. **Temporary vs. permanent:** A **temporary** policy shift is expected to be reversed in the near future → does **not** affect the long-run expected exchange rate $E^e$. A **permanent** shift changes $E^e$ (via PPP or current account dynamics).

2. **Small open economy:** Domestic policy does not affect $R^*$ or $P^*$.

## Temporary Monetary Expansion

**Shock:** One-time increase in $M^s$, expected to be reversed (temporary).

**Effect on curves:** $E^e$ unchanged (temporary) → AA shifts **up** (depreciation) by the money supply effect only; DD unchanged.

**Short-run result:**

$$M^s \uparrow \implies R \downarrow \implies E \uparrow \text{ (depreciation)} \implies \frac{EP^*}{P} \uparrow \implies CA \uparrow \implies Y \uparrow$$

New equilibrium: **higher $E$ (depreciation) and higher $Y$** — both the exchange rate depreciates and output expands.

**Diagram:** $AA^1 \to AA^2$ (shifts up); new intersection with $DD$ at higher $(Y^2, E^2)$.

## Temporary Fiscal Expansion

**Shock:** $\uparrow G$ or $\downarrow T$, expected to be temporary.

**Effect on curves:** DD shifts **right** (higher aggregate demand); AA unchanged (no change in $M^s$, $R^*$, or $E^e$).

**Short-run result:**

$$G \uparrow \implies Y \uparrow \implies L(R,Y) \uparrow \implies R \uparrow \implies E \downarrow \text{ (appreciation)}$$

New equilibrium: **lower $E$ (appreciation) and higher $Y$**.

**Crowding out mechanism:** The fiscal expansion raises output → demand for money rises → domestic interest rate rises → capital inflow → exchange rate appreciates → net exports fall (partially offsetting the fiscal stimulus). This is **exchange rate crowding out** — the fiscal multiplier in an open economy with perfect capital mobility is smaller than in a closed economy.

**Diagram:** $DD^1 \to DD^2$ (shifts right); new intersection with $AA$ at lower $(Y^2, E^2)$.

## Policies to Maintain Full Employment

When a temporary shock pushes $Y$ below full-employment $Y^f$, policymakers can respond:

| Shock | Passive response | Active monetary response | Active fiscal response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temporary fall in foreign demand (DD left) | $Y < Y^f$ persists | $M^s \uparrow$ → AA up → restores $Y^f$ at higher $E$ | $G \uparrow$ → DD right → restores $Y^f$ at lower $E$ |
| Temporary rise in money demand (AA down) | $Y < Y^f$, $E \downarrow$ | $M^s \uparrow$ → AA restored → $Y^f$ at original $E$ | $G \uparrow$ → DD right → $Y^f$ but $E$ falls further |

**Key insight:** Monetary and fiscal policy have **different effects on the exchange rate**:
- Monetary expansion → depreciation
- Fiscal expansion → appreciation

## Permanent Monetary Expansion

**Shock:** Permanent $M^s$ increase.

**Additional channel vs. temporary:** Under PPP, a permanent increase in money supply eventually raises prices proportionally → long-run $E^e$ rises proportionally (more depreciation expected).

**Effect on AA:** The AA curve shifts **more** than in the temporary case — both the money supply effect and the expectation effect push AA up.

**Short-run result:**
1. Economy moves from point 1 (original) to point 2: $Y > Y^f$, $E$ rises substantially
2. Since $Y > Y^f$: prices rise over time ($P \uparrow$)
3. Higher $P$ → lower real money supply → AA shifts back down
4. Long-run equilibrium (point 3): $Y = Y^f$, $E^3 > E^1$ (permanently more depreciated)

**Permanent monetary neutrality:** In the long run, a permanent $M^s$ increase leaves real output unchanged but raises $E$ and $P$ proportionally — consistent with the quantity theory of money and relative PPP.

## Permanent Fiscal Expansion

**Shock:** Permanent $G \uparrow$ (from long-run equilibrium).

**Additional channel:** A permanent fiscal expansion changes long-run CA and therefore the long-run equilibrium exchange rate. Specifically, higher $G$ → higher absorption → CA deficit → **expected long-run depreciation reduced** (or appreciation required to equilibrate CA in the long run) → $E^e \downarrow$.

**Effect on AA:** $E^e \downarrow$ → AA shifts **down** (appreciation for every $Y$).

**Combined effect:**
- DD shifts **right** (direct fiscal stimulus)
- AA shifts **down** (expectation effect)

**Result:** If the economy starts at long-run equilibrium ($Y = Y^f$), the permanent fiscal expansion produces:
- **No change in output** ($Y$ stays at $Y^f$)
- **Immediate and permanent appreciation** ($E$ falls)

The appreciation of the exchange rate **exactly offsets** the fiscal stimulus on aggregate demand — complete crowding out via the exchange rate. The DD and AA shifts are designed so that the new intersection of $DD^2$ and $AA^2$ is at the same $Y^f$ but lower $E$.

**Policy implication:** Permanent fiscal expansion is **ineffective** at raising output in a small open economy with perfect capital mobility and flexible exchange rates — the exchange rate fully crowds out net exports. This result is related to the **Mundell-Fleming model** conclusion under flexible exchange rates.
