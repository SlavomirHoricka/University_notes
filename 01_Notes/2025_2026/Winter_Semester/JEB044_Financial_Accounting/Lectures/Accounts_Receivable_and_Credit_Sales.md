# Accounts Receivable and Credit Sales

## Definition

**Accounts Receivable (AR)** represents the amounts owed to a firm by customers who have purchased goods or services on credit. AR is a **current asset** on the balance sheet, classified as a financial asset.

## Journal Entry for a Credit Sale

A credit sale involves two pairs of entries — one to record the revenue and one to match the cost:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts Receivable | Selling Price | |
| Sales Revenue | | Selling Price |
| Cost of Goods Sold | Purchase Cost | |
| Inventory | | Purchase Cost |

Revenue is recorded at the **selling price**; inventory is removed at its **carrying (purchase) cost**. The difference between the two is the gross profit on the transaction.

## Net Realizable Value (NRV)

AR is reported on the balance sheet at **Net Realizable Value**:

$$NRV = AR_{\text{gross}} - \text{Allowance for Uncollectible Accounts (AUA)}$$

NRV represents the amount management expects to actually collect. Because some customers will inevitably default, gross AR overstates the true asset value; the allowance corrects for this.

## Cash Collection Entry

When the customer pays:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | Amount Received | |
| Accounts Receivable | | Amount Received |

## Relationship to Sales Returns

Customers may also return goods or receive allowances for defective items. These reduce net sales and, if already collected, reduce cash or AR. See [[Sales_Returns_and_Allowances]] for the full treatment.

## Analytical Ratios

**Accounts Receivable Turnover:**

$$\text{AR Turnover} = \frac{\text{Net Credit Sales}}{\text{Average AR}}$$

**Days Sales Outstanding (DSO):**

$$DSO = \frac{365}{\text{AR Turnover}} = \frac{\text{Average AR}}{\text{Net Credit Sales} / 365}$$

A rising DSO may signal deteriorating credit quality or looser credit standards.

## Allowance for Uncollectible Accounts

Because not all receivables will be collected, financial accounting requires the **Allowance Method** to estimate and pre-recognise bad debt losses. See [[Allowance_for_Uncollectible_Accounts]] for the full treatment including IS Approach, BS Approach (aging schedule), and the contrast with the Direct Write-Off Method.
