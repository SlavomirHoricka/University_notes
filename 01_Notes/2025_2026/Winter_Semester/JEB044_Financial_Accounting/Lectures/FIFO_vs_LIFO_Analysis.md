# FIFO vs. LIFO — Real-World Analysis and Managerial Incentives

## Why Firms Choose LIFO

1. **Economic accuracy of COGS:** LIFO matches recent (higher) costs against current revenues, avoiding the overstatement of gross profit from holding gains embedded in old inventory layers.
2. **Tax minimisation:** Higher COGS → lower taxable income → lower tax payments in inflationary environments. This is the primary economic rationale for US firms.
3. **Shareholder alignment in high-debt firms:** firms with significant leverage have shareholder-manager alignment around tax minimisation (Maloney et al., *Journal of Business*, 1993).

## Why Firms Choose FIFO

1. **Physical accuracy:** most goods physically flow FIFO (perishables, dated goods), so FIFO matches physical reality.
2. **Higher reported earnings:** FIFO COGS is lower → higher NI → higher manager bonuses and better optics for equity investors.
3. **Cleaner balance sheet:** FIFO ending inventory is close to current market value; LIFO inventory can be severely understated.
4. **LIFO reserve manipulation:** LIFO reserve liquidation (selling more than buying) can inflate income in a given year, creating an earnings management tool. FIFO avoids this.
5. **IFRS comparability:** international investors and firms filing under IFRS cannot use LIFO. A firm wishing to report under IFRS must abandon LIFO, requiring two sets of books.

## Regulatory Landscape

| Standard | FIFO | LIFO | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IFRS** | Allowed | **Prohibited** (2003) | IAS 2 banned LIFO for international comparability |
| **US GAAP** | Allowed | Allowed | LIFO conformity rule: tax LIFO = financial LIFO |

## Industry Distribution (S&P 500, 2022)

- ~55% of S&P 500 companies use **FIFO**
- ~15% use **LIFO**
- Remainder use average cost or combinations

LIFO is most prevalent in: **oil & gas, chemicals, metals, auto parts** — capital-intensive, commodity-driven industries with large inventory balances (~$320bn LIFO inventory in S&P 500 as of 2022).

## Trend: LIFO → FIFO Switches

Approximately 30 large US companies switched from LIFO to FIFO in 2021–2022. Drivers:
- **US Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA, 2017):** corporate rate cut from 35% to 21% reduced the tax benefit of LIFO (smaller difference between LIFO and FIFO tax).
- **JIT inventory management:** just-in-time reduces inventory holding periods, narrowing the timing gap between FIFO and LIFO valuations, making the difference less material.
- **IFRS convergence pressure:** multinational firms choosing to unify global reporting.

## The LIFO Reserve as an Analytical Tool

Analysts convert LIFO financial statements to FIFO for cross-firm comparison:

$$Iv_{\text{FIFO}} = Iv_{\text{LIFO}} + LFR$$

$$\text{Pre-tax Income}_{\text{FIFO}} = \text{Pre-tax Income}_{\text{LIFO}} - \Delta LFR$$

$$\text{NI}_{\text{FIFO}} \approx \text{NI}_{\text{LIFO}} - \Delta LFR \times (1 - \text{tax rate})$$

Where $LFR = Iv_{\text{FIFO}} - Iv_{\text{LIFO}}$ is disclosed in the financial statement footnotes.

## Managerial Incentives Summary

| Factor | Pushes Towards FIFO | Pushes Towards LIFO |
|---|---|---|
| Compensation tied to NI | ✓ (higher NI) | |
| High corporate tax rate | | ✓ (larger tax savings) |
| High debt / creditor pressure | | ✓ (align with shareholders on tax) |
| International reporting (IFRS) | ✓ (required) | |
| JIT manufacturing | ✓ (difference immaterial) | |
| Large, old inventory layers | | ✓ (large LIFO reserve = large tax shield) |

The interplay of these factors explains why inventory accounting choice is an equilibrium outcome of the firm's operating model, tax environment, governance structure, and reporting jurisdiction.
