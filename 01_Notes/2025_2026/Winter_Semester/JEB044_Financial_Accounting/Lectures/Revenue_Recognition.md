# Revenue Recognition

## Historical Framework (FASB SFAC No. 5)

Under US GAAP's conceptual framework, revenue is recognised when it is:
1. **Earned** — the entity has substantially completed its performance.
2. **Realised or Realisable** — the entity has received cash or has a claim to cash (receivable) that is convertible.

This two-condition test was largely replaced in practice by IFRS 15 and ASC 606.

## IFRS 15 — The Five-Step Model

IFRS 15 *Revenue from Contracts with Customers* (effective 2018) provides a single principles-based framework for all industries. Revenue is recognised when (or as) **control** of a good or service transfers to the customer — replacing the older IAS 18 risk-and-rewards model.

### Step 1 — Identify the Contract

A contract must have commercial substance, approved terms, identified parties, and a collectibility assessment. No contract = no revenue recognition.

### Step 2 — Identify Performance Obligations

A **performance obligation** is a promise to transfer a distinct good or service. A good or service is distinct if:
- the customer can benefit from it on its own (capable of being distinct), and
- it is separately identifiable from other promises in the contract (distinct within the contract).

Bundled packages (e.g., phone + 2-year service plan) must be separated into individual performance obligations.

### Step 3 — Determine the Transaction Price

The **transaction price** is the amount the entity expects to receive in exchange for transferring goods/services. Adjustments include:
- **Variable consideration** (discounts, rebates, refunds) — estimated using expected value or most likely amount.
- **Time value of money** — if the contract contains a significant financing component (payment deferred >1 year), the transaction price is discounted.
- **Non-cash consideration** — measured at fair value.

### Step 4 — Allocate the Transaction Price

The transaction price is allocated to individual performance obligations in proportion to their **standalone selling prices** (SSPs). If an SSP is not directly observable, it must be estimated.

### Step 5 — Recognise Revenue When (or As) Performance Obligation Is Satisfied

Revenue is recognised when control transfers:
- **Point in time:** typically when delivery occurs, title passes, or the customer accepts the asset.
- **Over time:** if the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits (e.g., subscriptions, long-term construction contracts) — recognise using percentage-of-completion.

$$\text{Revenue recognised in period} = \frac{\text{Costs incurred to date}}{\text{Total estimated costs}} \times \text{Total contract price}$$

## Special Cases

### Credit Sales

Revenue is recognised at the point of sale (performance obligation fulfilled). The receivable records the future cash claim:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts Receivable | $+\text{Selling Price}$ | |
| Sales Revenue | | $+\text{Selling Price}$ |
| COGS | $+\text{Cost}$ | |
| Inventory | | $-\text{Cost}$ |

### Gift Cards / Unearned Revenue

Cash received in advance creates a **liability** (unearned revenue). Revenue is recognised only when the performance obligation is satisfied (gift card is redeemed):

- Cash receipt: Dr Cash / Cr Unearned Revenue
- Redemption: Dr Unearned Revenue / Cr Sales Revenue

### Warranties as Performance Obligations

Under IFRS 15, a warranty that provides the customer with a service beyond assurance of conformance is a separate performance obligation. A portion of the transaction price is allocated to the warranty and recognised over the warranty period.

## IFRS 15 vs. IAS 18

| Dimension | IAS 18 (old) | IFRS 15 (current) |
|---|---|---|
| Core test | Transfer of risks and rewards | Transfer of control |
| Framework | Rules-based | Principles-based |
| Multi-element | Sparse guidance | Explicit 5-step model |
| FASB alignment | Different | Converged (ASC 606 ≈ IFRS 15) |

## Entities Most Affected by IFRS 15

- Firms offering **bundled packages** (telecom, software): must allocate revenue across obligations.
- **Licensing** arrangements: point-in-time vs. over-time recognition can differ substantially.
- **Long-term contracts** (construction, defence): percentage-of-completion vs. completion method.
- **Variable pricing** (royalties, performance bonuses): estimation uncertainty in transaction price.
