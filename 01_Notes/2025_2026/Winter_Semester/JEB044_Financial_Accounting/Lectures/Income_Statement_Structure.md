# Income Statement Structure

## Purpose

The Income Statement (IS) measures **value generation** over a reporting period. It reports revenues earned and expenses incurred under the **accrual basis**, regardless of cash timing.

$$NI = \text{Net Sales} - COGS - SG\&A \pm \text{Other Operating} \pm \text{Financial Items} - \text{Income Tax}$$

## Multi-Step Income Statement

The multi-step format reveals several subtotals that are analytically important:

```
Net Sales (NS)
  – Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
= Gross Profit (GP)
  – Selling, General & Administrative Expenses (SG&A)
  ± Other Operating Items
= Operating Income (EBIT)
  ± Financial Items (interest income/expense)
= Pre-tax Income (EBT)
  – Income Tax Expense
= Net Income (NI)
```

Formally:

$$EBIT = NS - COGS - SG\&A - \text{Other Operating Expenses}$$

$$NI = EBIT \pm \text{Financial Items} - \text{Tax}$$

## Key Line Items

### Net Sales

Net Sales = Gross Sales – Sales Returns and Allowances (SRA) – Sales Discounts.

SRA is a **contra-revenue** account (debit-normal) that reduces reported revenue without deleting the original sale entry. See [[Sales_Returns_and_Allowances]].

### Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

COGS represents the carrying amount of inventory items sold during the period. Its calculation depends on the cost flow assumption chosen (FIFO, LIFO, Average Cost). See [[Inventory_Cost_Flow_Assumptions]].

$$COGS = \text{Beginning Inventory} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Ending Inventory}$$

### Gross Profit and Gross Margin

$$GP = NS - COGS$$

$$\text{Gross Margin} = \frac{GP}{NS}$$

Gross margin is a key indicator of pricing power and production efficiency.

### SG&A

Selling, General, and Administrative expenses are **period costs** — they are expensed as incurred, not matched to specific units of revenue. Examples: marketing, executive salaries, office rent.

### EBIT

Earnings Before Interest and Taxes (EBIT) measures **operating profitability** independent of capital structure and tax regime. Used in valuation (EV/EBIT multiple) and in ROA computation.

### Net Income and Retained Earnings

Net income flows into the **Statement of Equity** and updates Accumulated Retained Earnings (ARE):

$$ARE_t = ARE_{t-1} + NI_t - Dv_t$$

where $Dv_t$ are dividends declared in period $t$.

## Income vs. Cash Flow

Net income $\neq$ cash generated. The difference arises from:
1. **Non-cash expenses** (depreciation, amortisation) — reduce NI but not cash.
2. **Accruals** — revenues recognised before cash receipt (accrued revenue); expenses recognised before cash payment (accrued expense).
3. **Deferrals** — cash received before revenue is earned (unearned revenue); cash paid before expense is recognised (prepaid).

The [[Statement_of_Cash_Flows]] reconciles NI to operating cash flows via the indirect method. The fundamental long-run equivalence is:

$$\sum_{t=1}^{\infty} NI_t = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} CF_t$$

Period-by-period, income and cash flows diverge; cumulatively over a firm's life, they converge.
