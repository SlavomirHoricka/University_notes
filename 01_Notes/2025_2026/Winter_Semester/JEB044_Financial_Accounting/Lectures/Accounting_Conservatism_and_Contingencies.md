# Accounting Conservatism and Contingencies

## Accounting Conservatism

**Conservatism** is a qualitative characteristic of financial reporting requiring that when uncertainty exists, assets and income should not be **overstated** and liabilities and expenses should not be **understated**.

Practically: recognise losses as soon as they are probable; recognise gains only when they are realised or virtually certain.

Conservatism explains several accounting rules:
- The **Allowance Method** for bad debts (recognise expected losses before they are confirmed).
- **Lower of Cost or Net Realisable Value** for inventory.
- **Impairment testing** for assets (write down if fair value < carrying value, never write up unless specific conditions met).
- **IAS 37** provisions for contingent liabilities.

## Contingencies under IAS 37

A **contingency** is an existing condition whose outcome depends on one or more uncertain future events. IAS 37 prescribes different accounting treatments based on the probability of the contingent outflow.

### IAS 37 Probability Table

| Probability Descriptor | Approximate Threshold | Financial Accounting Treatment | Tax Accounting Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Virtually certain | > 95% | Provide (recognise on BS/IS) | Recognise |
| Probable | > 50% | Provide (recognise); detailed footnote | Footnotes only |
| Possible | > 5% | Footnote disclosure | Ignore / footnote |
| Remote | < 5% | Ignore | Ignore |

### Definitions

- **Provision:** a liability of uncertain timing or amount that is recognised on the balance sheet when the outflow is probable and the amount can be reliably estimated.
- **Contingent liability:** a possible obligation whose existence is uncertain (possible) — disclosed in footnotes but not recognised on the balance sheet.
- **Contingent asset:** a possible asset — disclosed only when virtually certain (conservatism prevents premature recognition of gains).

### Worked Example

A company is being sued for $500,000.

| Scenario | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Legal counsel says loss is virtually certain (>95%) | Recognise provision: Dr Litigation Expense 500k / Cr Provision for Litigation 500k |
| Legal counsel says loss is probable (>50%) | Recognise provision + detailed footnote |
| Outcome is possible (>5%) | Disclose in footnotes only; no balance sheet entry |
| Outcome is remote (<5%) | No disclosure required |

## Reliability vs. Relevance in Contingency Accounting

Tax accounting demands **reliability** — it requires a direct write-off only when the loss is confirmed (to prevent firms from suppressing taxable income by recording speculative provisions). Financial accounting demands **relevance** — it requires provisions for probable losses to give investors a forward-looking picture of solvency. This creates a systematic wedge between book income and taxable income. See the same tension in [[Allowance_for_Uncollectible_Accounts]].
