The first topic asks how to fix terms for an exchange that will take place later. Waiting for a future spot transaction leaves its price unknown; a forward agrees a price now, while an exchange-traded futures contract adds standardized specifications. Standardization allows equivalent opposite trades to close positions, and clearing interposes a formal counterparty between traders. This gives liquidity and a mechanism for managing counterparty exposure, while clients still face the source's explicit warning about their own broker's default. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_1/L10(EssentialsOfFuturesTrading).pptx|L10(EssentialsOfFuturesTrading).pptx, slide 2–5, 7, 12]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_1/FI01(Futures Contracts).doc|FI01(Futures Contracts).doc, Ch. I, LibreOffice-rendered p. 2–5]]

A long benefits from a rise in the futures price and a short from a fall. Daily marking to market transfers these gains/losses into margin accounts. Initial margin is a deposit rather than the asset's purchase price; maintenance is the minimum balance; a call requires replenishing the account to the specified target. Small deposits create leverage and can require substantial subsequent funding. The connected explanation and the handout's five-day table are in [[01_Notes/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_1/Margins_and_Marking_to_Market.md|Margins and marking to market]]. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_1/L10(EssentialsOfFuturesTrading).pptx|L10(EssentialsOfFuturesTrading).pptx, slide 5–6]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_1/FI01(Futures Contracts).doc|FI01(Futures Contracts).doc, Ch. I, LibreOffice-rendered p. 4, 9]]

Price hedging follows from combining these daily gains/losses with a spot purchase or sale. With the convention $B_t=S_t-F_t$, a matched purchase hedge costs $Nq(F_0+B_t)$, and a matched sale hedge produces $Nq(F_0+B_t)$. At delivery the source's convergence/no-arbitrage argument gives $B_T=0$, fixing the effective opening futures price. An earlier spot transaction leaves basis risk. [[01_Notes/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_1/Basis_Convergence_and_Futures_Hedging.md|Basis, convergence and futures hedging]] derives these equations and separates the lecture's deferred-payoff value illustration from its daily-settlement account. Week 2 asks how standardized futures terms turn quotation changes into money flows across four classes of financial assets. These are the lecture's hypothetical tutorial contracts; their specifications and exchange list are source examples, rather than current trading information. The index contract multiplies index points by EUR 10 and settles in cash. The currency contract trades EUR 25,000 at a dollar-per-euro quotation and can deliver currency. Their common calculation converts quotation changes into ticks, then multiplies by tick value and contract count. The long benefits from a quotation rise, and the short from a fall. Returns shown on initial margin use a small deposit as denominator, which explains the source's large simply annualized figures. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 2]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 3]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 4]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 5]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 6]]

The short-rate contract adds an inverse quotation, $F=100-i_{\%}$, and a deposit accrual period. A long gains when the implied rate falls. For EUR 500,000 over a quarter-year, a one-basis-point change is worth EUR 12.50. Futures gains can supplement a lower future spot deposit rate, while physical delivery compensation bridges an arranged rate to its benchmark. The notes preserve the source's quarter-year versus 91/365 and payment-timing qualifications rather than treating its 9.5% equality as universally exact. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 7]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 8]]

Bond futures add a notional reference bond and the short's delivery choices. Their USD 0.01 quoted-price tick is worth USD 10 on USD 100,000 face value. A conversion factor adjusts the clean futures invoice for a particular eligible bond, and accrued coupon completes the full invoice. Financing the bond purchase and holding it until delivery creates carry. The source's positive-carry rule selects late delivery, while negative carry selects early delivery. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 9]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 10]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 11]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 12]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_2/L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx|L11(ExamplesOfFinancialFutures).pptx, slide 13]]

The implied repo rate compares the full purchase outflow with delivery proceeds and any coupon received and reinvested before delivery. The source defines the cheapest-to-deliver bond as the eligible bond with maximum IRR. The formulas are derived step by step in the focused note. Two exact numerical equalities in the source do not reproduce their printed inputs: the conversion-factor example's 171-day exponent conflicts with its 170-day diagram/result, and the IRR example's 43/180 accrual does not yield its stated 3.57%. These source errors are explicit and should be separated from reliable formula practice. ### Assessment and the handbook's cutoff ambiguity

The handbook allocates **20 points to seminar activity** and **80 to the written exam**. At least **50% of the final exam**, hence 40 out of 80, is needed for eligibility to pass. It says there are three attempts and that an exam score cannot be improved after the minimum requirement has been passed. It also says at least 50 total points are needed; however, its grade table labels **0–50 as F** and starts **E at 51**. The exact treatment of a total of 50 is therefore unresolved in the supplied handbook and should be clarified with the teaching assistant; it is not silently chosen here. The table gives E=51–60, D=61–70, C=71–80, B=81–90 and A=91–100. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_1/handbook-1.pdf#page=3|handbook-1.pdf, PDF p. 3–4]]

Seminars begin with a brief theory recap and around three worked board exercises, then allow roughly 15–20 minutes for a point-bearing problem. The handbook gives up to three points per seminar, capped at 20 overall, and asks students to bring the formula list and a calculator. Question sets are supplied in SIS, while solutions are not. The written exam includes 40 theory points and four exercises worth 10 each; full exercise credit requires careful steps and explanation. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_1/handbook-1.pdf#page=4|handbook-1.pdf, PDF p. 4]]

Only a non-programmable calculator is allowed in the exam. The handbook prohibits communication devices, books, own notes, teamwork and exchange of notes, and says the formula list will be provided. SIS registration is compulsory. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Financial_Markets_Instruments_I/Week_1/handbook-1.pdf#page=4|handbook-1.pdf, PDF p. 4–5]]
