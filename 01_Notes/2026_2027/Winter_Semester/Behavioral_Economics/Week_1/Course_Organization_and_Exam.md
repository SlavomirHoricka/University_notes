## Teaching and preparation

Michal Bauer and Julie Chytilová teach the course. Teaching is in person. The introduction invites questions during lectures and at their end, and describes a sequence of theory or intuition, experimental evidence, and applications. Slides are posted in SIS and reading the papers is strongly encouraged. Microeconomics I (JEB104) is the prerequisite. ## Assessment format and dates

The grade is based on the written final test. The exam-information document sets a maximum of 100 points and an 80-minute duration. It includes multiple-choice questions with exactly one correct answer and open-ended text answers. Incorrect answers receive no negative points. The exam is closed book: the supplied rules prohibit supporting materials, devices, calculators, and AI tools, while allowing blank paper and pens. Students must agree to the academic-integrity declaration for their test to be evaluated; refusal is graded F. ## Boundary of the Week 1 notes

Topic 1 asks for the definition of behavioral economics, its difference from experimental economics, and the course overview and requirements. The syllabus's later topics and bibliographies are a map of the rest of the course. The SIS export marks the misperceptions topic “if time allows,” whereas the syllabus lists it without that qualification; neither statement changes the selected Week 1 material. No later-topic readings have been ingested in this pass. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Behavioral_Economics/Week_1/2026_Behavioral_Economics_Syllabus.pdf#page=2|Syllabus, PDF pp. 2–5]] [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Behavioral_Economics/Week_1/Předměty3.pdf#page=2|SIS export, PDF pp. 2–3]]

The sample test includes inequality aversion, the trust game, statistical discrimination, the ultimatum game, comparisons of discrimination models, and a question about intergroup contact referring to Corno et al. (2022). These are examples of the final exam's scope and question formats. Their substantive answers require later-topic material and are deliberately excluded from this Week 1 introduction. The uploaded example document supplies questions and answer options, not an answer key.
