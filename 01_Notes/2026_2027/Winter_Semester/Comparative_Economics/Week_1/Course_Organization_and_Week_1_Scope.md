## Course question and teaching format

The course compares the features of economic systems, the behavior they encourage, and their implications for performance. It emphasizes institutions and their interaction with performance and stability. The course-level overview includes market economies, central planning, worker-managed companies, transition, state capitalism, and informal institutions. These descriptions establish the course's larger setting; they do not add later-week lessons to this ingestion. The existing [[01_Notes/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Comparative_Economics/Week_1/Comparing_Economic_Systems.md|systems-comparison note]] develops the Week 1 classification and measurement questions. ## Assessment

The course expects continuous work, active and honest teamwork, and participation in the midterm and final exam. Four brief summaries or critical evaluations of assigned texts are written during seminars in weeks 3, 6, 9, and 12. Each contributes 5% of the grade, for 20% in total. The week-7 midterm contributes 20%, and the final test contributes 60%. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Comparative_Economics/Week_1/Předměty.pdf#page=2|SIS export, PDF pp. 2–3]]

The final test includes multiple-choice questions, problem sets using models and graphs, and a brief essay chosen from provided topics. The export says that creative thinking and understanding receive more weight than memorization. Original written work is required. The printed grading ranges are A 91–100%, B 81–90%, C 71–80%, D 61–70%, E 51–60%, and F 0–50%; the document does not provide a separate fractional-score rounding rule.
