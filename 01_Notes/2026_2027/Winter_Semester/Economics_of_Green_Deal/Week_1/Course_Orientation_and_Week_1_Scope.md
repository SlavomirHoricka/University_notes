# Course Orientation and Week 1 Scope

What will Economics of Green Deal examine, and what does the first week's uploaded material actually explain? The course studies environmental problems and climate change through economics, including how optimal regulation is defined and how policies' economic, social and environmental impacts can be quantified. It gives special attention to decarbonisation scenarios and pathways, technology and social innovation. This note records that orientation and the assessment information, while identifying which Week 1 topics are supported by the supplied files. ## The course's central question

The English annotation frames the European Green Deal as a policy to transform the EU into a carbon-neutral, resource-efficient and competitive economy. The transition involves technology, behaviour and lifestyle, and the course asks how to assess its impacts on the economy, environment and people. Environmental economics supplies the foundation, complemented by energy, transport and behavioural economics, energy-system modelling and environmental sciences. These are the course's stated aims and framing, rather than separately verified claims about current policy outcomes. [[00_Materials/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Economics_of_Green_Deal/Week_1/Předměty.pdf#page=1|Předměty.pdf, physical PDF p. 1, English annotation]]

The introductory [[01_Notes/2026_2027/Winter_Semester/Economics_of_Green_Deal/Week_1/Environment_Economy_Nexus|environment–economy framework]] explains why that assessment has several dimensions. Resources and environmental services support economic activity, waste feeds back into the environment, and both consumption and environmental amenities affect utility. Consequently, the course's stated environmental, economic and social impact questions share a common conceptual setting. This connection is a synthesis of the annotation and the introductory diagrams. ## Assessment and grading in the supplied materials

The deck and SIS export agree on three components. A writing test with a satisfactory grade carries **65 points**, the written open-book examination carries **35 points**, and active participation in lectures carries **10 points**. The open-book exam is described as testing understanding and application rather than memorisation. The source qualifies permitted material as “all, but electronic,” which excludes electronic materials under its stated wording. It does not enumerate particular permitted paper resources.
